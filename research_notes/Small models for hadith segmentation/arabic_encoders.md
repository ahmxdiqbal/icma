# Arabic and multilingual encoders for tagging spans in classical Arabic hadith entries (as of October 2026)

Scope: encoder models of roughly 100M to 600M parameters that a team could fine-tune as a BIO / span tagger to split one hadith entry into typed spans (chain, link to the Prophet, matn, the collector's formulas such as مثله، نحوه، بمعناه، بهذا الإسناد, the collector's comments, narrators' remarks), with output mapped back to exact character offsets. Vocabulary follows `GLOSSARY.md`: "chain" for isnad, "narrator", "collector", "collection".

Notation used below: "n/s" means the cited page does not state the value. "(inference)" marks my own reasoning rather than a sourced claim.

## 1. Which encoders are strongest for Arabic, and for Classical Arabic, as of October 2026?

### Takeaway
For vocalized classical text, the best-matched encoders are Arabic models whose pretraining includes classical sources: CAMeLBERT-CA (UAE lab, Apache-2.0, OpenITI, 512 tokens, trained on text with diacritics removed), and two 2026 releases: NeoAraBERT (Qatar/Lebanon, CC BY-SA 4.0, includes OpenITI, a tafsir dataset and a diacritized corpus, diacritics-aware tokenizer, 1,024 tokens, top scores on classical tasks against five Arabic baselines) and Rawi (Algeria, CC BY-NC-SA 4.0, AraModernBERT further trained on 985M tokens of OpenITI pre-modern prose, 8,192 tokens, no downstream results yet). AraBERTv0.2 and ARBERTv2 stay strong general baselines at 512 tokens with no diacritic support. The multilingual long-context encoders (mmBERT, EuroBERT, mGTE, BGE-M3) solve the length problem but have generic tokenizers and no published Arabic token-tagging results; mmBERT is slightly behind XLM-R on multilingual NER.

#### Head-to-head evidence on which Arabic encoder is strongest
- CAMeLBERT's main finding: "the variant proximity of pre-training data to fine-tuning data is more important than the pre-training data size" ([paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.06678)).
- NeoAraBERT (ACL Findings, July 2026) ran AraBERTv2, ARBERTv2, MARBERTv2, AraModernBERT (NAMAA) and CAMeLBERT-mix through one fine-tuning pipeline on 23 tasks; NeoAraBERT checkpoints won 18; average 83.79 (Mix) vs 81.04 for the best baseline (AraModernBERT) ([paper](https://aclanthology.org/2026.findings-acl.1293/)). CAMeLBERT-CA was not among the baselines.
- Rawi card, pseudo-perplexity per word on 197 held-out classical biographical entries: Rawi 13.68, CAMeLBERT-CA 13.63 (at 512 context), AraModernBERT base 26.83, AraBERTv0.2 32.34, mmBERT-base 42.59 ([card](https://huggingface.co/Al-Sila/Rawi)).
- mmBERT paper, Arabic XNLI accuracy: mmBERT base 74.5, mmBERT small 71.4, EuroBERT-210m 66.8 ([paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.06888v1)).
- mDeBERTa-v3-base averages 79.8 on zero-shot XNLI vs 76.2 for XLM-R base ([card](https://huggingface.co/microsoft/mdeberta-v3-base)).

### Inferences
- Ranking for this task among non-American models (inference from the data match and the evidence above): (1) CAMeLBERT-CA as the safe baseline, with input diacritics removed; (2) NeoAraBERT-Mix, the only model trained to read diacritics and the strongest on classical benchmarks, but with a 1,024-token cap and a heavy input pipeline; (3) Rawi or AraModernBERT for native 8,192-token context, with Rawi closest to classical prose but untested downstream and non-commercial; (4) AraBERTv0.2 / ARBERTv2 as MSA controls; (5) EuroBERT-210m, mGTE and BGE-M3 as multilingual long-context fallbacks. American options (XLM-R-large, mmBERT, mDeBERTa) are useful controls, not first choices.
- CAMeLBERT-CA's tokenizer keeps diacritics while its vocabulary was trained on diacritic-free text, so raw vocalized input would likely produce many `[UNK]` or broken pieces (inference from the card's preprocessing description and `do_lower_case: false`; not tested).
- Rawi's card treats OpenITI share-alike terms as binding on the weights, while CAMeLBERT-CA, also trained on OpenITI, is Apache-2.0. The two positions conflict; for research and non-commercial use either works.
- The AraModernBERT paper and the NAMAA checkpoint share the same configuration (22 layers, 768 hidden, 1,152 intermediate, 50,280 vocab, 8,192) and the NAMAA affiliation, so they describe the same model (inference; the paper does not link the checkpoint).

### Gaps
- Licence of AraBERT, AraELECTRA and ARBERTv2 weights: none found on cards or GitHub. Treat as research-only until the authors confirm.
- Whether NeoAraBERT loads with stock `transformers` or needs remote code, and whether its stemming and diacritic-separation pipeline ships with the checkpoint: not found.
- Whether CAMeLBERT's 30k vocabulary contains harakat at all: not verified (needs a vocab check).
- JABER's max sequence length: not stated in the pages read. SABER and Char-JABER weights: not released per the GitHub page.
- No encoder pretrained directly on the Shamela corpus turned up in searches; OpenITI-based models (CAMeLBERT-CA, Rawi, SarahSav, NeoAraBERT) are the closest.
- Arabic coverage of mGTE (75 languages) and BGE-M3 parameter count: not confirmed on the pages read.
- A community "AraEuroBERT-210M" (MIT) appeared in search results ([listing](https://www.promptlayer.com/models/araeurobert-210m)); I did not verify what it is.

## 2. Reported results on Arabic token classification, and on Classical Arabic specifically

### Takeaway
Arabic encoders land within a few F1 points of each other on MSA NER and POS, and rankings flip between papers and setups. Classical-text evidence is thin but consistent: CAMeLBERT-CA leads on hadith NER (97.78 F1 on CANERCorpus, though all ten models exceed 96%), and NeoAraBERT's diacritic handling gives large gains on diacritized classical poetry tasks. No published, evaluated encoder exists for chain/matn segmentation; the one Hugging Face segmenter reports no metrics. In the two Quran shared tasks, fine-tuned encoders (AraELECTRA, AraBERT, CAMeLBERT-CA) won.

### Cited Findings

#### CAMeLBERT variants (Inoue et al. 2021), F1
- NER, ANERcorp (MSA): MSA 82.4, Mix 80.8, DA 74.1, CA 67.9; MSA-1/8 82.6 ([card](https://huggingface.co/CAMeL-Lab/bert-base-arabic-camelbert-ca)).
- POS, PATB (MSA): MSA 98.3, Mix 98.1, CA 97.8; POS ARZTB (Egyptian): MSA/Mix 93.6, CA 92.3; POS Gumar (Gulf): CA 97.7, MSA/DA 97.9 ([card](https://huggingface.co/CAMeL-Lab/bert-base-arabic-camelbert-ca)).

#### Classical Arabic and hadith
- CANERCorpus: an expert-annotated corpus of 7,000 hadiths from Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī with 20 entity classes, built by Salah and Zakaria ([IJACSA paper](https://thesai.org/Downloads/Volume16No10/Paper_19-Evaluating_Transformer_Based_Pretrained_Models.pdf); corpus on [GitHub](https://github.com/RamziSalah/Classical-Arabic-Named-Entity-Recognition-Corpus); the paper splits this URL across a line break, so the exact form is reconstructed and was not opened).
- Muhammed and Azab (Cairo University, IJACSA vol. 16 no. 10, 2025) fine-tuned ten encoders on CANERCorpus: all F1 above 96%; CAMeLBERT-CA-NER best at 97.78 (P 97.51, R 98.05); ArBERT 97.47; Arabic-BERT 97.06; MARBERT 96.97; multilingual BERT lowest at 96.53 ([paper](https://thesai.org/Downloads/Volume16No10/Paper_19-Evaluating_Transformer_Based_Pretrained_Models.pdf)).
- Their setup: diacritics, punctuation and special characters removed; text cut into 38-word segments; random 80/10/10 split; max length 256; LR 1e-5; batch 16; one Tesla T4 on Colab ([paper](https://thesai.org/Downloads/Volume16No10/Paper_19-Evaluating_Transformer_Based_Pretrained_Models.pdf)). The authors note results may not carry to other classical genres.
- Alshammari, Atwell and Alsalka (2023) applied four off-the-shelf Arabic NER models (CAMeLBERT-CA, Hatmimoha, Marefa-NER, Stanza) to a new 5,000-word Bukhārī test set built on CANERCorpus labels and got "disappointing F1-scores" ([paper](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/211347)).
- Chain/matn segmentation: SHK4K/hadith-segmentation-bert fine-tunes AraBERTv0.2 with B/I-SANAD and B/I-MATN tags, caps input at 512 subwords, recommends stripping excess tashkeel, MIT licence, no training data or metrics reported ([card](https://huggingface.co/SHK4K/hadith-segmentation-bert)).
- pyHadith (Umar Butler, 2020, GPL-3.0, now removed from PyPI) used spaCy 2.2.4 models trained on 102,153 annotated hadiths from sunnah.alifta.gov.sa and reported chain/matn segmentation P 0.9709, R 0.9961, F 0.9833 on 20,430 held-out hadiths ([libraries.io](https://libraries.io/pypi/pyHadith)).
- Tarmom, Atwell and Alsalka (ICON 2020) segmented chain and matn with PPM compression models: 92.76% and 90.10% accuracy in two experiments (figures from search snippets of [the paper](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/172041); not opened).
- Qur'an QA 2023 (ArabicNLP 2023): in the reading-comprehension task (extract answer spans from Qur'an passages), the top run scored pAP 0.5711 (TCE, self-ensembled AraELECTRA/AraBERT fine-tuned first on Arabic TyDiQA); the GPT zero-shot team was second at 0.5457; all other teams used encoders. The best retrieval run (MAP 0.2505) ensembled CAMeLBERT-CA and AraBERTv0.2 dual encoders. The organisers conclude "encoder-based transformer models are still taking the lead over generative transformer models" ([overview](https://aclanthology.org/2023.arabicnlp-1.76)).
- IslamicEval 2025, subtask 1A, asks for the character-offset spans of intended Qur'an verses and hadiths inside LLM answers, scored by macro F1. Results: Burhan AI 90.06 (fine-tuned gpt-4.1-mini plus agent tools), HUMAIN 87.20 (LLM sequence-to-sequence bracket tags aligned back to the text by dynamic programming), TCE 86.11 (few-shot Qwen-235B / GPT-4o plus fuzzy matching), Isnad AI 66.97 (rule-based); majority baseline 36.17 ([overview](https://aclanthology.org/2025.arabicnlp-sharedtasks.67)).

### Inferences
- For hadith text the choice among Arabic encoders is likely worth one or two F1 points, while in-domain labels are worth far more: off-the-shelf models failed on Bukhārī text, and every model fine-tuned on CANERCorpus exceeded 96%.
- The 97.78 figure is probably optimistic: a random split of one collection lets the same narrators and phrases appear in train and test. A split by collection or by meaning group is the honest test for our data.
- Chain/matn boundaries are mostly signalled by fixed phrases (قال رسول الله، عن النبي, the collector's formulas), so a fine-tuned encoder should do well; the pyHadith 0.98 F with old spaCy models supports this (inference).
- IslamicEval shows that LLM pipelines can do character-offset span detection well, but no fine-tuned encoder was entered, so it says nothing about encoders vs LLMs on equal footing.

### Gaps
- No published evaluation of any encoder on chain / link-to-Prophet / matn / collector-formula segmentation with metrics.
- No published Arabic-specific NER or POS numbers for EuroBERT, mmBERT, mGTE or BGE-M3.
- No study found that fine-tunes and tests encoders on fully vocalized hadith input; CANERCorpus work removed diacritics.
- Per-team details of IslamicEval systems beyond the overview were not read.

## 3. The sequence-length problem: what people do, and which encoders reach 1,024 to 8,192 tokens

### Takeaway
In the project's own corpus the 512-token cap is a tail problem: the median entry is 66 words and the 99th percentile is 397 words, so roughly 1 to 2% of entries exceed 512 tokens when diacritics are stripped, and perhaps 5 to 10% if a tokenizer splits off every diacritic (estimate). Sliding windows with overlap are built into Hugging Face's token-classification pipeline. Native long context comes from ModernBERT-style encoders (mmBERT, AraModernBERT, Rawi: 8,192), EuroBERT (8,192), mGTE and BGE-M3 (8,192), NeoBERT (4,096) and NeoAraBERT (1,024).

### Cited Findings
- Local corpus, `hadiths.nass` in the hadith-kg database (plain text of chain plus matn): 715,790 entries from 425 collections; characters median 431, p90 1,027, p99 2,705, max 39,778; words median 66, p90 154, p99 397, max 5,479, mean 87.0 ([local DB](../../data/raw/hadith-kg/hadith-kg.db), queried read-only 2026-10-19).
- Same corpus: entries above 200 words 5.3%; above 300 words 1.95%; above 350 words 1.35%; above 500 words 0.59%; above 1,000 words 0.11% ([local DB](../../data/raw/hadith-kg/hadith-kg.db)).
- Same corpus: 98.95% of entries carry diacritics; 0.761 diacritic marks per Arabic letter overall, 0.833 in the median entry ([local DB](../../data/raw/hadith-kg/hadith-kg.db)).
- Tokens per word for Arabic: AraBERT v2 1.555, GPT-4's cl100k 3.689, on a 114,882-word test set (self-reported on the DF-Arc card, not peer-reviewed) ([card](https://huggingface.co/dataflare/df-arc/blob/main/README.md)).
- NeoAraBERT's authors state that diacritics-aware tokenization and stemming "increase token counts and effectively shorten the usable context length" ([paper](https://aclanthology.org/2026.findings-acl.1293/)).
- Hugging Face `TokenClassificationPipeline` `stride`: "the text is split into chunks of size model_max_length. Works only with fast tokenizers and `aggregation_strategy` different from `NONE`"; the model shifts forward by `model_max_length - stride` tokens each step ([docs, transformers v5.20.1](https://huggingface.co/docs/transformers/main_classes/pipelines)).
- The same docs show the QA pipeline's overlap defaults: `doc_stride` 128 with `max_seq_len` 384 ([docs](https://huggingface.co/docs/transformers/main_classes/pipelines)).
- AraModernBERT's authors: in Arabic NLP "most systems rely on chunking long documents to fit short-context encoders"; their model processes 8,192 tokens in one pass, and MLM perplexity improved from 25.54 at 512 tokens to 21.05 at 8,192 ([paper](https://aclanthology.org/2026.abjadnlp-1.39)).
- Context lengths: mmBERT 8,192 ([paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.06888v1)); ModernBERT 8,192 ([card](https://huggingface.co/answerdotai/ModernBERT-base)); EuroBERT 8,192 ([card](https://huggingface.co/EuroBERT/EuroBERT-210m)); NeoBERT 4,096 ([card](https://huggingface.co/chandar-lab/NeoBERT)); mGTE 8,192 ([paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.19669)); BGE-M3 8,192 ([card](https://huggingface.co/BAAI/bge-m3)); AraModernBERT and Rawi 8,192 ([paper](https://aclanthology.org/2026.abjadnlp-1.39); [card](https://huggingface.co/Al-Sila/Rawi)); NeoAraBERT 1,024 ([paper](https://aclanthology.org/2026.findings-acl.1293/)); CAMeLBERT, AraBERT, ARBERTv2, XLM-R 512 ([CAMeLBERT card](https://huggingface.co/CAMeL-Lab/bert-base-arabic-camelbert-ca); [NeoAraBERT Table 1](https://aclanthology.org/2026.findings-acl.1293/); [mmBERT paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.06888v1)).
- AraModernBERT keeps the ModernBERT design: global attention every third layer, 128-token sliding-window attention elsewhere, RoPE theta 160,000 (global) and 10,000 (local) ([AraModernBERT paper](https://aclanthology.org/2026.abjadnlp-1.39)).

### Inferences
- At about 1.5 tokens per word with diacritics stripped, 512 tokens holds about 340 words, so roughly 1.4% of entries (about 10,000) need windowing. If each diacritic sequence becomes its own token (NeoAraBERT style) or diacritics split words into more pieces, the cutoff falls near 170 words and roughly 7 to 9% of entries exceed it (estimates from the word distribution; real counts need a tokenizer run).
- For a 512-token model, a window of 512 with stride 128 to 256, keeping each token's label from the window where it sits farthest from an edge, is the standard fix. The split between chain and matn usually appears early in an entry, so window seams rarely fall at the boundary that matters most (inference).
- A long-context encoder removes windowing code and lets a late collector's formula (بهذا الإسناد مثله) see the whole entry. That argues for running one 8,192-token model (Rawi or AraModernBERT, or EuroBERT / mGTE) against one 512-token Arabic model as the main comparison.

### Gaps
- Actual token counts per entry for each candidate tokenizer on vocalized text were not measured; no tokenizer library is installed locally and I did not download tokenizer files.
- No published study compares windowed 512-token tagging with native long-context tagging on Arabic.

## 4. Encoders vs small generative LLMs for span extraction / NER when both are fine-tuned

### Takeaway
Fine-tuned encoders match fine-tuned 7B LLMs on Arabic NER (WojoodNER 2024: AraBERTv2 slightly ahead of fine-tuned BLOOMZ-7b and Gemma-7b), and in biomedical NER fine-tuned 8B LLMs gain 1 to 8 F1 at 40 to 220 times the inference time. Prompted LLMs without fine-tuning trail fine-tuned encoders badly (AdabNER: 0.59 vs 0.86 F1). Generative taggers also need an extra alignment step to recover character offsets, which encoders get for free.

### Cited Findings
- DRU team at WojoodNER 2024 fine-tuned BLOOMZ-7b-mt, Gemma-7b and AraBERTv2 for multi-label token classification: "the three fine-tuned models performed similarly with AraBERTv2 favored (F1: Flat=.8780 Nested=.9040)"; a five-model ensemble reached .8782 / .9043 ([paper](https://aclanthology.org/2024.arabicnlp-1.104/)). Per-model LLM scores are not in the abstract.
- AdabNER (Mourad and Jarrar, ACL 2026; nested NER on MSA literary books, about 876K tokens): fine-tuned AraBERTv2 F1 0.86 (stratified) and 0.83 (leave-book-out); five LLMs with few-shot prompting only, best Gemini 3 Pro at 0.59 ([paper](https://aclanthology.org/2026.acl-long.1541/)).
- Obeidat, Al Nahian and Kavuluru (University of Kentucky, 2025): QLoRA-tuned Mistral-7B and Llama-3.1-8B vs fine-tuned 336M to 435M encoders (BERT-large, BiomedBERT, DeBERTa-v3-large) on five biomedical datasets with BIO tags. LLMs led on four datasets (for example JNLPBA 75.67 vs 73.59; BC5CDR 90.73 vs 88.86) and trailed on BioRED (88.58 vs 89.08) ([paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.00664v1)).
- Same study, seconds per test sample: BERT about 0.026 to 0.044, DeBERTa about 0.048 to 0.067, Mistral-7B 1.94 to 11.55, Llama-8B 1.71 to 9.64; the best encoder was "40 times faster" on JNLPBA and "nearly 220 times faster" on BioRED; encoders ran on one H100, the LLMs needed two ([paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.00664v1)).
- Yano and Takamura (BioNLP 2026): seven decoder LLMs (Gemma, Qwen2.5, Llama 3.1, Llama 3.2) with causal attention replaced by full attention and label-supervised training "outperforms encoder baselines (BERT, RoBERTa, DeBERTa)" with a suitable LoRA setup, but "does not reach SOTA on BC5CDR and CoNLL2003" ([paper](https://aclanthology.org/2026.bionlp-1.25/)).
- Ettin (Weller et al., JHU, 2025), paired encoders and decoders from 17M to 1B trained with one recipe: "encoder-only models excel at classification and retrieval tasks while decoders excel at generative tasks"; a 400M encoder beats a 1B decoder on MNLI; converting one into the other by continued training is "subpar" ([paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.11412)).
- Nielsen, Enevoldsen and Schneider-Kamp (ScandEval, 2024): "encoder models can achieve significantly better NLU performance than decoder models despite having orders of magnitude fewer parameters" across eight European languages ([paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.13469)).
- GLiNER, a compact bidirectional encoder for NER, reports "outperforming both ChatGPT and fine-tuned LLMs in zero-shot evaluations" and parallel extraction as "an advantage over the slow sequential token generation of LLMs" ([paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.08526)).
- Qur'an QA 2023: encoders won the span-extraction task; a zero-shot GPT system was second ([overview](https://aclanthology.org/2023.arabicnlp-1.76)). IslamicEval 2025: LLM pipelines took the top three places in character-offset span detection, with no encoder entry reported ([overview](https://aclanthology.org/2025.arabicnlp-sharedtasks.67)).
- HUMAIN's IslamicEval system had to align LLM bracket-tagged output back to the source text by dynamic programming ([overview](https://aclanthology.org/2025.arabicnlp-sharedtasks.67)); the biomedical study mapped LLM predictions back to the dataset tokenization before scoring ([paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.00664v1)).

### Inferences
- For a fixed label set and plenty of training data, a 100M to 300M encoder is the cheaper default: a comparable fine-tuned 4B to 8B LLM might gain a point or two at one to two orders of magnitude more compute per entry.
- A generative tagger must rewrite or reference the input, and Arabic diacritics make exact copying fragile; any drift breaks offset mapping and needs an alignment step. An encoder labels the given tokens and cannot drift.
- The local Hugging Face cache already holds Qwen3.5 0.8B, 4B and 9B checkpoints (observed in `~/.cache/huggingface/hub`), so a like-for-like LoRA test against an encoder on the same labels would be cheap to run.

### Gaps
- No Arabic or classical-Arabic study compares a fine-tuned encoder with a fine-tuned small LLM (0.5B to 4B) on span extraction.
- Per-model F1 for the fine-tuned BLOOMZ and Gemma runs in the DRU paper is not in the abstract; the full paper was not read.
- The biomedical timing used unbatched per-sample inference; batched serving (for example vLLM) would narrow the gap, and I found no Arabic timing study.

## 5. Practical notes: inference speed for the corpus, training cost, Hugging Face support, character offsets

### Takeaway
Tagging the whole corpus is small work for an encoder: about 62M words in 715,790 entries, which a base-size encoder at the published RTX 4090 throughputs (90k to 180k tokens per second) would finish in well under an hour on one GPU. Fine-tuning fits on a single free-tier GPU. The corpus already carries matn offsets for 99.84% of entries, narrator-mention offsets and 117,194 Qur'an-quote spans, which can seed training labels. Offsets survive if every text change (diacritic removal, normalization) happens inside the fast tokenizer's normalizer; external preprocessors (ArabertPreprocessor, Farasa, NeoAraBERT's stemmer) return no offset map.

### Cited Findings

#### Speed
- ModernBERT paper, NVIDIA RTX 4090, thousands of tokens per second, short context (512 fixed / variable around 256): BERT-base 180.4 / 90.2; RoBERTa-base 179.9 / 89.9; DeBERTa-v3-base 70.2 / 35.1; ModernBERT-base 148.1 / 147.3; BERT-large 54.4 / 27.2; DeBERTa-v3-large 24.6 / 12.3; ModernBERT-large 52.3 / 52.9. Long context (8,192 fixed / variable around 4,096): ModernBERT-base 123.7 / 133.8; GTE-en-MLM-base 46.1 / 23.4 ([paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.13663v2)).
- mmBERT base is "more than 2x faster" than earlier multilingual encoders on variable-length input and about 4x faster on long context; mmBERT small is about 2x faster than base ([paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.06888v1)).
- AraModernBERT needs about 6.8 GB of GPU memory for 8k-token inference ([paper](https://aclanthology.org/2026.abjadnlp-1.39)).
- Corpus size: 715,790 entries, mean 87.0 words ([local DB](../../data/raw/hadith-kg/hadith-kg.db)).

#### Training cost
- CANERCorpus fine-tuning of ten encoders ran on one Tesla T4 on Google Colab ([paper](https://thesai.org/Downloads/Volume16No10/Paper_19-Evaluating_Transformer_Based_Pretrained_Models.pdf)).
- Rawi's domain-adaptive pretraining (311,397,956 tokens read, 1,200 steps) took 1.5 GPU-hours on one A100 40GB ([card](https://huggingface.co/Al-Sila/Rawi)).
- NeoAraBERT's fine-tuning recipe for all models: max length 512, batch 32, 5 epochs, AdamW, LR 2e-5, 10% warmup ([paper](https://aclanthology.org/2026.findings-acl.1293/)). mmBERT card example for classification: LR 3e-5, batch 32, 3 epochs, max length 512 ([card](https://huggingface.co/jhu-clsp/mmBERT-base)). EuroBERT paper suggests warmup 0.1, linear schedule, task LRs for the 210m model between 1.7e-5 and 7.7e-5 ([card](https://huggingface.co/EuroBERT/EuroBERT-210m)).
- Local silver labels: 714,610 of 715,790 entries (99.84%) have `matn_start`/`matn_end` character offsets into `nass`; `hadith_rawis` stores start/end offsets of inline narrator mentions; `hadith_ayas` holds 117,194 Qur'an-quote spans with start/end ([local DB](../../data/raw/hadith-kg/hadith-kg.db), schema and counts queried 2026-10-19).

#### Hugging Face support
- Native `transformers` classes: ModernBERT has `ModernBertForTokenClassification` (covers mmBERT, AraModernBERT, Rawi) ([docs](https://huggingface.co/docs/transformers/model_doc/modernbert)); CAMeLBERT, AraBERT and ARBERT are plain BERT checkpoints and load with the standard BERT classes ([BERT docs](https://huggingface.co/docs/transformers/model_doc/bert); BERT architecture per [JABER paper Table 1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.10687)).
- Remote code: EuroBERT needs `trust_remote_code=True` and transformers 4.48 or later ([card](https://huggingface.co/EuroBERT/EuroBERT-210m)), and its modeling file defines `EuroBertForTokenClassification` ([modeling_eurobert.py](https://huggingface.co/EuroBERT/EuroBERT-210m/blob/main/modeling_eurobert.py)); mGTE's `Alibaba-NLP/new-impl` code defines `NewForTokenClassification` ([modeling.py](https://huggingface.co/Alibaba-NLP/new-impl/blob/main/modeling.py)); NeoBERT loads with `trust_remote_code=True` ([card](https://huggingface.co/chandar-lab/NeoBERT)).
- JABER is not supported by `transformers` and needs its GitHub code ([card](https://huggingface.co/huawei-noah/JABER)).

#### Character offsets
- `TokenClassificationPipeline` returns `start` and `end` indices into the input; "If you want to have the exact string in the original sentence, use `start` and `end`"; they exist only "if the offsets are available within the tokenizer" ([docs](https://huggingface.co/docs/transformers/main_classes/pipelines)).
- In transformers v5.20.1, `BertTokenizer` is "backed by HuggingFace's tokenizers library"; the old Python class is now `BertTokenizerLegacy` ([docs](https://huggingface.co/docs/transformers/model_doc/bert)).
- The Rust `NormalizedString` "keeps both version of the string, alignments information between both"; parts of the original can be retrieved "by indexing it with offsets from the normalized one" ([docs.rs](https://docs.rs/tokenizers/latest/tokenizers/tokenizer/normalizer/struct.NormalizedString.html)). Pre-tokenizer splits carry spans in the original sentence, which become the encoding's offsets ([tokenizers docs](https://huggingface.co/docs/tokenizers/pipeline)).
- BERT tokenizers: `strip_accents` "If this option is not specified, then it will be determined by the value for `lowercase`" ([docs](https://huggingface.co/docs/transformers/model_doc/bert)); the tokenizers docs build BERT-style accent removal as `NFD()` followed by `StripAccents()` ([tokenizers docs](https://huggingface.co/docs/tokenizers/pipeline)).
- Unicode: Arabic fatha U+064E is a "Nonspacing Mark (Mn)" ([compart](https://www.compart.com/en/unicode/U+064E)); أ U+0623 decomposes canonically to ا U+0627 plus ◌ٔ U+0654 HAMZA ABOVE ([compart](https://www.compart.com/en/unicode/U+0623)).
- `ArabertPreprocessor.preprocess()` returns a plain string with no offset map ([preprocess.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/aub-mind/arabert/master/preprocess.py)); AraBERT v2 also needs Farasa segmentation ([GitHub](https://github.com/aub-mind/arabert)); NeoAraBERT's pipeline inserts `[+]` segment markers and reorders diacritics after each bare word ([paper](https://aclanthology.org/2026.findings-acl.1293/)).

### Inferences
- Speed estimate: about 62.3M words times about 1.5 tokens per word is roughly 93M tokens. At 90k tokens per second (BERT-base, variable length, RTX 4090) that is about 17 minutes; ModernBERT-base at about 147k tokens per second is about 11 minutes. Even three times the tokens from diacritics stays near one hour. These numbers come from English benchmarks on an NVIDIA card and are estimates for this corpus, not measurements.
- Turning on BERT's `strip_accents` would remove harakat but would also turn أ إ آ ؤ ئ into their bare carriers, because NFD splits off the hamza or madda as a nonspacing mark (follows from the Unicode data above; verify with a one-line test). A `Replace` normalizer limited to U+064B to U+0652, U+0670 and tatweel U+0640 removes only diacritics and keeps offsets aligned through `NormalizedString`.
- Label at word level (first subword carries the label), then convert word labels to character spans with `offset_mapping`. Chain/matn boundaries fall between words, so word-level labels lose nothing (inference).
- The source's matn offsets are silver labels, not gold: whether they put the link to the Prophet (قال رسول الله) and the collector's formulas inside or outside the matn needs a check against the Pavlovitch gold study before training on them.
- The brief mentions about 400,000 documents; the local database holds 715,790 entries. Throughput scales linearly either way.

### Gaps
- No reliable published absolute CPU throughput for BERT-base found; only relative int8 speedups (up to about 2.9x on Intel VNNI CPUs, per a Microsoft ONNX Runtime post seen only as a search snippet) ([Microsoft blog](https://opensource.microsoft.com/blog/2021/03/01/optimizing-bert-model-for-intel-cpu-cores-using-onnx-runtime-default-execution-provider/)). Apple-silicon (MPS) throughput for these encoders was not found either.
- Throughput for EuroBERT, NeoAraBERT and mGTE on Arabic was not found.
- Whether the mDeBERTa and XLM-R fast tokenizers give clean offsets on vocalized Arabic (for example, diacritics split into separate pieces with overlapping offsets) was not tested.
