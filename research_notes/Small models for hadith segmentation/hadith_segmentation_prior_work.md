# Prior work on splitting hadith entries into chain and matn, finding chains in classical Arabic, and extracting narrators (to October 2026)

Conventions used in these notes. "Chain" means isnad. Paper titles, dataset field names and quotations keep their own wording (Sanad, Isnad, transmitter, book). "Local check" marks numbers I computed myself from data files during this research; the query and file are named so they can be re-run. Where a number comes only from a secondary source, the bullet says so.

## 1. Which papers have split hadith entries into chain (isnad) and matn, with what methods, results and labelled data?

### Takeaway
Published splitters are mostly rule-based or shallow statistical systems, tested on 100 to 500 hadiths from the six canonical collections, with metrics that cannot be compared across papers. The best documented cross-collection result is about 92.5% "accuracy" (Altammami), and that metric counts a split as correct when it is off by fewer than 4 tokens. Transformer and LLM work exists (an AraBERT token tagger, CAMeLBERT/AraT5 chain parsers, LLM corpus pipelines), but none of it is a published, evaluated splitter for entries that hold several chains, several matns and collector comments.

#### Details that matter for reuse

- Altammami's accuracy metric is lenient: a split is "correct if it is off by three words", formally |actual chain tokens − produced chain tokens| < 4, justified because narrator names "usually do not exceed three tokens". The same metric is used for all approaches in the thesis. ([Altammami thesis, section 4.5.4](https://etheses.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/32802/1/Altammami_SH_Computing_PhD_2023.pdf))
- Thesis breakdown on the 500 test hadiths (Table 4.16): look-up lists 88.3% normalized / 87.1% not; ML 92.5% / 92.3%. On 450 regular hadiths: 92.0 / 90.5 (look-up) and 95.1 / 95.1 (ML). On 50 irregular hadiths: 56.0 / 56.9 (look-up) and 79.0 / 68.6 (ML). ([Altammami thesis](https://etheses.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/32802/1/Altammami_SH_Computing_PhD_2023.pdf))
- The 2020 classifier was trained on 4,686 segmented al-Bukhari hadiths (314,340 bigram instances), scored 94.9% on a 30% held-out split and 88.6% on the 36,218 bigrams of the evaluation data. Its rule: a split point A where a Matn token is followed by three more Matn tokens; if five Isnad tokens follow A, find the next Matn run and set split point B, which handles an entry with a second chain before the matn. ([LREC 2020](https://aclanthology.org/2020.lrec-1.415.pdf))
- Thesis classifier comparison for labelling bigrams (Table 4.11): Multinomial Naive Bayes 95.6%, SVM 91.4%, logistic regression 95.9%, random forest 96.9%. ([Altammami thesis](https://etheses.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/32802/1/Altammami_SH_Computing_PhD_2023.pdf))
- The thesis HMM used a 6-tag set (begin/inside/end for chain and for matn), the pomegranate library, 6,278 training hadiths, 97% token accuracy on 1,570 validation hadiths. Its errors on the test set fall into three kinds: vague split points, narrator descriptions inside the chain labelled as matn, and entries with "several Isnad and Matn segments", because the algorithm cuts at the last end-of-chain tag. The author concludes that hadith "can be segmented to fine-grained segments that go beyond two segments". ([Altammami thesis, sections 4.7 to 4.8](https://etheses.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/32802/1/Altammami_SH_Computing_PhD_2023.pdf))
- The 2019 and 2020/2023 "92.5%" figures belong to different systems on different test sets: 222/240 with look-up lists (2019), and the normalized ML system on 500 hadiths (thesis). The same look-up approach scored 88.3% on the larger 500-hadith set. ([WACL 2019](https://aclanthology.org/W19-5605.pdf); [thesis](https://etheses.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/32802/1/Altammami_SH_Computing_PhD_2023.pdf))
- Boella's HedExtractor, as described by Altammami: finds each hadith by its number, transliterates, treats words between transmission words as narrator names, and starts the matn when no transmission word appears within the next 100 characters, a threshold "chosen based on trial and error". ([Altammami thesis, section 4.2](https://etheses.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/32802/1/Altammami_SH_Computing_PhD_2023.pdf))
- Harrag's FST extracted Num-Kitab, Title-Kitab, Num-Bab, Title-Bab, Num-Hadith, Isnad, Matn, Taalik and Atraf, and assumed the matn is surrounded by parentheses, so it depends on one edition's punctuation. ([Altammami thesis, section 4.2](https://etheses.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/32802/1/Altammami_SH_Computing_PhD_2023.pdf))
- Altammami's verdict on prior work: rule-based systems scored highest but were built for one collection; Siddiqui's ML system dropped 14 points on a second collection; and the results "are not comparable" because test data and metrics differ. Most prior datasets were never released. ([Altammami thesis, section 4.2](https://etheses.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/32802/1/Altammami_SH_Computing_PhD_2023.pdf))
- Tarmom's labelled data: the NAH corpus has 452,624 words from lesser-known collections downloaded from islamweb.net and almeshkat.net, with per-hadith fields including Isnad, Matan and "Authors Comments". For training, chains and matns were "manually extracted" from N1 (732 hadiths; 52,221 chain words and 33,489 matn words). The metrics are word-level (a confusion matrix of Isnad/Matan words), not per entry. ([Tarmom et al. 2020](https://aclanthology.org/2020.icon-main.4.pdf))
- Tarmom relabelled LK's "the Prophet, may God bless him and grant him peace, said" from matn to chain "for consistency", and labelled stories about a narrator as chain but stories setting the scene of the Prophet's words ("chronology story") as matn. The two corpora put the boundary in different places. ([Tarmom et al. 2020](https://aclanthology.org/2020.icon-main.4.pdf))
- Najeeb's 2022 pipeline does not split raw text: "The raw Hadiths will be divided into Matn and Isnad; then, the Isnad-Phrase extraction process will be conducted by an expert scientist in Hadith science." The model only assigns one of eight categories (Prophet-Name, Narrator-Name, Narrator-Name-Prefix, Received-Method, Received-Method-Prefix, Replacement, Title, Others) to each pre-cut phrase. Average accuracy: gazetteer 75%, HMM 83%, hybrid 86%. ([Najeeb 2022](https://doi.org/10.1155/2022/7160509), full text via Wiley)
- Najeeb's 2020 genetic-algorithm approach to the same tagging task "achieved 81.44% accuracy" on Sahih Muslim. ([Najeeb 2020, IEEE Access](https://doi.org/10.1109/ACCESS.2020.2968417))

#### Transformer and LLM work, 2023 to 2026

- Oumer 2025 (MSc, University of Malta) treats the chain as a parsing problem, not a split. Data: 276,347 hadiths extracted from 33 collections on the Ifta' Sunnah platform, 223,478 kept, split 70/15/15. Test results: narrator recognition CAMeLBERT-CA P/R/F1 0.98/0.99/0.98, AraT5 0.97/0.97/0.97, AraBART 0.78/0.94/0.84; teacher-student links ignoring which chain: CAMeLBERT-CA 0.96/0.93/0.95, AraT5 0.96/0.95/0.95; links tagged with a chain number ("chain-aware" triplets): AraT5 0.94/0.93/0.93, AraBART 0.76/0.37/0.48. ([GitHub README](https://github.com/JehadOumer/Automatic-Hadith-Isnad-Parsing-Models); [thesis record](https://www.um.edu.mt/library/oar/handle/123456789/137629))
- The Oumer announcement says the models are "applied to raw Arabic isnad text", and that alternative chains are "often marked by phrases and symbols". The thesis abstract describes "annotated narrator mentions, and structured narration chains" from "an authoritative digital source" and does not mention hand annotation. ([SIGARAB post, April 2026](https://groups.google.com/g/sigarab/c/XFnOZYvgAfg); [thesis record](https://www.um.edu.mt/library/oar/handle/123456789/137629))
- Rezwan (Asgari-Bidhendi et al., arXiv, 4 Oct 2025) uses "prompt-engineered" LLMs, not named, to find where each narration starts and ends, separate chain from matn, and validate against the original pages by fuzzy matching. Corpus: "over 1.2M narrations", mainly from Maktabat Ahl al-Bayt. Six experts scored 1,213 sampled narrations on a 0-10 scale: chain/matn separation 9.33 in the abstract but 9.30 in Table 1; overall 8.46 versus 3.66 for the Noor corpus. No inter-rater agreement and no release are reported; the paper also gives corpus sizes of 1.5M and 2.3M in places and calls the system both Rezwan and Najm. ([arXiv 2510.03781](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.03781); [HTML full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.03781v1))
- GTAF (Greentech Apps Foundation, December 2025 blog) reports an "AI-powered" narrator-chain extractor at 95% average accuracy on 20 hadiths from the Muwatta (15 of 20 perfect). Weak points named: a descriptor (مولى) taken as a narrator, parallel narrators, vague references such as "a man". Method and scoring rule are not described. ([GTAF blog](https://gtaf.org/blog/evaluating-the-performance-of-our-narrator-chain-extraction-system-for-hadith-analysis/))
- A NeurIPS 2025 "Muslims in ML" affinity poster (Kaymaram, Nassabi, Akar) proposes an LLM framework for narrator name extraction and claims gains over traditional ML; the abstract gives no models, data or numbers. ([NeurIPS page](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2025/133006))
- faisalkindi/isnad (May 2026) uses Claude to parse a pasted chain into names and per-link formulas, and says it "Handles taḥwīl (ح), multi-compiler (قالا), and relative refs (عن أبيه)". No evaluation. ([GitHub README](https://github.com/faisalkindi/isnad))
- A 2025 Indonesian journal paper describes "sanad-matn segmentation" for digital takhrij with "a high degree of accuracy" but gives no numbers in its abstract. ([Nizamiyah 2025](https://doi.org/10.64691/nizamiyah.v1i4.125))

#### Reviews that frame the field

- Mhamedi, Mghari & El Hibaoui (TELKOMNIKA 24(3), June 2026) review 50+ studies and tabulate extraction results (their Table 4), but do not cover Muther & Smith or LLM work. ([TELKOMNIKA review](https://telkomnika.uad.ac.id/index.php/TELKOMNIKA/article/download/27447/12280))
- Haque, Islam et al. (arXiv, June 2026) say "segmentation tasks have matured" but list narrow corpora, weak benchmark comparability, synthetic-to-real gaps and sparse expert validation as limits; they report no metric values. ([arXiv 2608.20364](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.20364); [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.20364))

### Inferences
- No shared benchmark exists. Test sets run from 100 to 500 hadiths, mostly al-Bukhari or the six collections; metrics range from word-level accuracy to a ±3-token tolerance to phrase-category accuracy. A team result should be reported with exact-boundary accuracy and span-level F1 so it can be read against these.
- Every published splitter models one boundary, or at most a second chain before a single matn (Altammami's point B). None outputs separate spans for several chains, several matns and collector comments. Oumer's chain-aware triplets keep parallel chains apart but start from chain text, not from a whole entry.
- Altammami's irregular subset (79.0% at best, with tolerance) is the closest published proxy for the team's hard cases and shows how much harder they are than regular entries (95.1%).
- Reusable pieces: the LK 500-hadith test set as an outside check for single-boundary splitting; Oumer's CAMeLBERT/AraT5 models for narrator mentions inside chain text; Altammami's bigram approach or SHK4K's tagger as cheap baselines.

### Gaps
- I could not read the primaries of Boella 2011, Harrag 2011/2014, Maraoui 2018/2019, Siddiqui 2014, Mahmood 2018 or Makhlouta 2012. Their numbers above come from secondary sources that disagree with each other.
- Oumer's thesis is restricted access; whether any of his labels were hand-checked is not stated.
- I found no peer-reviewed paper that fine-tunes AraBERT/CAMeLBERT to split raw entries and reports results on multi-chain entries.
- The NeurIPS 2025 paper's full text sits behind an OpenReview verification page; contents unknown.
- Mosa's 2026 review "Charting the computational frontier in hadith sciences" (Neural Computing and Applications, [doi](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00521-026-12188-8)) and Kamran, Butt & Basharat 2026 ([SAGE](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/22104968261431425)) were not read; no abstracts were available.

## 2. Datasets: what they contain, how chain/matn boundaries were made, whether they were hand-checked, and known quality problems

### Takeaway
None of the large datasets has hand-checked chain/matn boundaries at scale. Sanadset's tags come from regular expressions with no reported evaluation and fail often outside the canonical collections. LK's boundaries come from Altammami's segmenter, with only al-Bukhari hand-checked. hadith-kg's single matn span is inherited from markup in a 2017 database dump, and follow-up chains, formulas and comments after it are unlabelled. Smaller curated resources exist (LK al-Bukhari, the NAH corpus, Multi-IsnadSet for Muslim, KITAB's annotated OpenITI sections, Muther's Ta'rikh Dimashq chains).

### Cited Findings

#### Sanadset 650K (Mghari, Bouras & El Hibaoui, Data in Brief 44, 2022, article 108540)
- 650,986 records "collected from 926 historical Arabic books of hadith"; fields: diacritized text with tags SANAD, NAR (each narrator) and MATN; Book; hadith number; Matn; list of narrators; number of narrators. ([Europe PMC full text](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC9440281/fullTextXML); [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9440281/))
- How boundaries were made: components separated "with the help of regular expressions and text matching technics"; the rules are not listed. Scraped with Beautiful Soup and Selenium from unnamed "public and free Islamic websites". No manual verification, error rate or accuracy is reported. 159,558 hadiths have no Sanad. ([Europe PMC full text](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC9440281/fullTextXML))
- Hosting and licence: Mendeley Data, DOI 10.17632/5xth87zwb5, release 5 dated 2 November 2022, CC BY 4.0; an earlier "Sanadset 368K" release exists. ([Mendeley v5](https://data.mendeley.com/datasets/5xth87zwb5/5); [Mendeley v2](https://data.mendeley.com/datasets/5xth87zwb5/2))
- Mirrors: arbml/Sanadset on Hugging Face (October 2022, three parquet shards); freococo/650k_sanadset (February 2026) whose card says "cc0-1.0", which conflicts with CC BY 4.0 on Mendeley. ([arbml/Sanadset](https://huggingface.co/datasets/arbml/Sanadset); [freococo/650k_sanadset](https://huggingface.co/datasets/freococo/650k_sanadset))
- Local check on the arbml mirror, shard `train-00002-of-00003` (216,995 rows, mostly Ibn Abi Shayba, Ahmad, al-Tabarani, Abd al-Razzaq, Ibn Abi Hatim's and al-Tabari's tafsirs, Hilya, Ta'rikh Dimashq): 75,905 rows have Sanad_Length 0; 61,985 of those contain a transmission verb (حدثنا، حدثني، أخبرنا، أخبرني، ثنا، أنبأنا, after stripping diacritics) and 54,986 match a simple chain pattern (such a verb followed within 80 characters by عن، حدثنا or ثنا). Most "No SANAD" rows therefore appear to hold an untagged chain; this is a heuristic upper bound. 2,587 rows carry two or more SANAD tags and 5,627 contain a standalone ح. ([shard file](https://huggingface.co/datasets/arbml/Sanadset/tree/main/data), queried with DuckDB)
- Local check, failure examples from the same mirror: al-Tabari's tafsir #18852 has three chains joined by ح, and the whole entry is tagged MATN with Sanad "No SANAD"; al-Tabari #17971 leaves a six-narrator chain untagged and tags only "ابن عباس" (once more inside the matn), Sanad_Length 1; al-Nasa'i #4153 has three teachers in parallel (al-Qa'qa' b. Hakim, Sumayy, Ubayd Allah b. Miqsam) and the Sanad field flattens them into one 9-name chain. The tagging convention puts "قال رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم" in the MATN. ([arbml/Sanadset](https://huggingface.co/datasets/arbml/Sanadset), rows via the Hugging Face datasets-server and local parquet)
- Later uses: narrator linking (Wirahman 2026, below), Narrator2Vec (Mghari et al. 2024, [doi](https://doi.org/10.1007/s13369-023-08224-7)).

#### LK Hadith Corpus (Altammami, Atwell & Alsalka; Leeds and King Saud University)
- Scraped from sunnah.com, whose own isnad markup "was not annotated consistently": the Arabic chain is usually not separated, and sometimes only the Prophet's words count as matn. Boundaries were then produced by the authors' segmenter (92%). ([LREC 2020](https://aclanthology.org/2020.lrec-1.415.pdf))
- Size: 39,038 hadiths in Table 3 (Bukhari 6,633; Muslim 7,293; Nasa'i 5,680; Ibn Maja 10,082; Tirmidhi 4,209; Abu Dawud 5,141); the same paper's evaluation section says "33,359 Hadith records of Arabic and an aligned English translation"; more than 10M tokens. ([LREC 2020](https://aclanthology.org/2020.lrec-1.415.pdf))
- Fields (16 CSV columns) include Arabic_Hadith, Arabic_Isnad, Arabic_Matn, Arabic_Comment ("the scholar's comment on the authenticity") and grades. ([GitHub README](https://github.com/ShathaTm/LK-Hadith-Corpus); [LREC 2020, Table 2](https://aclanthology.org/2020.lrec-1.415.pdf))
- Hand-checking: al-Bukhari was "thoroughly checked against the book and the mistakes produced by the segmenter has been fixed", making it the gold standard; for the other five collections only the first 50 hadiths each were checked, giving an error rate of 7.6%. The LREC paper instead infers an 8% error rate from the 92% accuracy. ([Altammami thesis, section 5.5.2](https://etheses.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/32802/1/Altammami_SH_Computing_PhD_2023.pdf); [LREC 2020](https://aclanthology.org/2020.lrec-1.415.pdf))
- Entries that are only a chain ending "بهذا الإسناد" are kept as records with no matn and no English. ([LREC 2020, Example 4](https://aclanthology.org/2020.lrec-1.415.pdf))
- Availability: GitHub ShathaTm/LK-Hadith-Corpus, 110 stars, 31 forks, no licence file, last push 3 May 2023; the 500-hadith test set is at ShathaTm/Hadith_segmenter_testing_data and narrator-name training data at ShathaTm/Hadith_Narrator_Names. The LREC paper itself is ELRA CC-BY-NC. ([GitHub](https://github.com/ShathaTm/LK-Hadith-Corpus); [thesis footnotes 28 and 31](https://etheses.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/32802/1/Altammami_SH_Computing_PhD_2023.pdf); [LREC 2020](https://aclanthology.org/2020.lrec-1.415.pdf))

#### hadith-kg (emadjumaah/hadith-kg, Hugging Face, CC BY 4.0)
- Card: 715,790 hadith from 425 classical collections; 577,024 complete chains (4.4M edges); 49,819 narrator biographies; 127,863 jarh wa ta'dil statements by 1,015 critics; 20,745 meaning groups. Text carries "character-offset annotations for narrator mentions, matn boundaries, and Quran quotes"; the card does not say how they were produced and reports no validation. Some narrators are stubs whose "names were recovered from the hadith text itself". ([dataset card](https://huggingface.co/datasets/emadjumaah/hadith-kg))
- Local check, `meta` table of hadith-kg.db: `source` = "mongodump hadithdb/hadith (2017-10-12) + rawa_cat.csv"; `built_by` = "build_hkg.py + finalize_hkg.py"; `cleaned_at` = 2026-07-09. The schema comment on `matn_start, matn_end` reads "offsets of the matn (span.nass) in nass". The boundaries are therefore inherited from a span element in the upstream database's markup, not produced by a model in this release (my inference from the schema comment). The `hadiths.matn` column holds the grade label (صحيح، حسن …), not the matn. ([dataset](https://huggingface.co/datasets/emadjumaah/hadith-kg); file queried: `/Users/ahmediqbal/Documents/icma/data/raw/hadith-kg/hadith-kg.db`)
- Local check, quality counters stored in `meta`: matn_span_missing 1,180; broken_link_hadiths 6,351; mentions_unrepairable 8,309; mentions_still_null 6,302; mentions_still_unknown_rawi 2,028; mentions_repaired_from_chain 1,087. ([dataset](https://huggingface.co/datasets/emadjumaah/hadith-kg))
- Local check, what the single span leaves out: of 714,610 entries with a span, 202,664 have more than 20 characters after `matn_end`. Examples: al-Bukhari 285 ends "تابعه عمرو بن مرزوق عن شعبة مثله، وقال موسى حدثنا أبان … مثله" after the span; Muslim no. 37 (book_id 2) has a second chain with ح and "بمثله" after the span. Neither is labelled. 29,546 entries contain a standalone ح and 5,870 contain "بهذا الإسناد" (diacritized string match, approximate). 595 entries have matn_start = 0. ([dataset](https://huggingface.co/datasets/emadjumaah/hadith-kg))
- Local check, chains per entry in the `sanads` table: 307,205 entries with one chain, 62,943 with two, 16,845 with three, 8,371 with four, more beyond. Chains are stored as ordered narrator IDs, not text spans. ([dataset](https://huggingface.co/datasets/emadjumaah/hadith-kg))

#### Other datasets
- Multi-IsnadSet (Farooqi et al., Data in Brief 54, 2024, 110439): Sahih Muslim only; 7,748 hadith records, 14,155 chains, 2,092 narrators, 77,797 edges (the Results section says 63,643). Scraped from ihsanetwork.org, narrator details from muslimscholars.info, matched with fuzzywuzzy plus manual mapping; "manually curated" with a named domain expert. Different chains of one hadith are recorded separately, but the splitting rules are not described. Mendeley DOI 10.17632/gzprcr93zn.2; the dataset licence is not stated (article CC BY 4.0). ([Europe PMC full text](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC11096860/fullTextXML); [abstract](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.dib.2024.110439))
- Ifta' Sunnah datasets (Oumer): 276,347 hadiths and 20,957 narrators from 33 collections; three Kaggle releases (original corpus, ID-linked chain graph of 257,840 hadiths, NER plus relation tuples with a 223,478-hadith benchmark split). ([GitHub hub](https://github.com/JehadOumer/Ifta-Sunnah-Hadith-and-Narrators-Dataset); [Kaggle NLP release](https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/jehadoumer/ifta-hadith-with-narrators-entities-and-relations))
- NAH corpus (Tarmom et al.): 452,624 words with Isnad, Matan and "Authors Comments" fields; chains and matns of N1 separated by hand. Availability not stated in the ICON paper. ([Tarmom et al. 2020](https://aclanthology.org/2020.icon-main.4.pdf))
- AR-Sanad 280K (Mahmoud et al., Information 13(2), 2022): about 280K artificial chains labelled with 18,298 narrator identities (279,625 per the TELKOMNIKA review). ([OpenAlex abstract record](https://api.openalex.org/works/https://doi.org/10.3390/info13020055); [TELKOMNIKA review](https://telkomnika.uad.ac.id/index.php/TELKOMNIKA/article/download/27447/12280))
- ABCD (Ramzy et al., BDCC 7(3), 2023): 29K hadiths, 18K narrators, built for classifying hadiths by the narrator they go back to. ([doi](https://doi.org/10.3390/bdcc7030141))
- SHK4K/hadith (Hugging Face, June 2026): al-Bukhari and Muslim with sanad/matn columns; a search snippet says the separation was added by a BERT model. ([dataset](https://huggingface.co/datasets/SHK4K/hadith))
- KITAB annotated OpenITI sections: 50 texts, 163 tagged regions, 907,110 tokens, 3,071 chains (Muther & Smith 2020), released as `trainingDataChunks_7-28-20.json` (16 MB) with `isnadTaggedLocations*.json` in an MIT-licensed repository. ([Muther & Smith 2020](https://aclanthology.org/2020.wanlp-1.12.pdf); [GitHub](https://github.com/mutherr/isnadDetectionCode))
- KITAB derived data: "People versus Books" (Zenodo, July 2021, CC BY-NC-SA 4.0) holds chains tagged automatically by Muther's classifier on OpenITI release 2020.1.2, including Ibn Sa'd chains split into name positions; "Al-Ṭabarī's Direct Informants" (Zenodo, 2021) holds chains extracted from three works of al-Tabari with names split into up to twelve positions. ([People versus Books](https://zenodo.org/records/5074633); [Direct Informants](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.5533889))
- Muther, Smith & Savant 2023: 2,379 chains from Ta'rikh Madinat Dimashq that go back through Ibn Sa'd, 14,454 name mentions, 13,072 (about 90%) linked by hand to 44 individuals. ([JHNR article](https://jhnr.net/articles/10.25517/jhnr.v8i1.135); [code and data](https://github.com/mutherr/isnadNameDisambiguation))
- Kaggle "Hadith Narrators Dataset (+24K)" by fahd09 is a narrator table with teachers and students, not a chain/matn split (search snippet only). ([listing](https://baselight.app/u/kaggle/dataset/fahd09_hadith_narrators))

### Inferences
- hadith-kg's span is a usable weak label for the main matn of an entry, but everything after `matn_end` is unlabelled mixed material (extra chains, formulas, comments, narrator remarks). Treating "before matn_start = chain, after matn_end = nothing" as gold would teach a model the wrong thing on about 28% of entries.
- Sanadset's tags look unreliable for non-canonical collections and multi-chain entries. At most it can serve as noisy pretraining data after filtering.
- LK al-Bukhari is the only sizeable hand-checked chain/matn split found, and it has one boundary per entry with collector comments moved to a separate field by the website's layout.
- Datasets disagree on where the boundary sits (whether "the Prophet said" belongs to the chain), so mixing them without a shared convention will add label noise.

### Gaps
- Sanadset's websites are not named, and I profiled only one of the three mirror shards.
- The upstream "hadithdb" behind hadith-kg is not identified anywhere I could find; the build scripts are not published on the card.
- The LK corpus licence could not be confirmed; the GitHub repository has no licence file.
- I could not search Kaggle directly, so datasets with explicit chain/matn columns there are not surveyed beyond the ones named.
- AR-Sanad's generation procedure (which narrator lists and teacher-student links were used) could not be checked because MDPI returned 403.

## 3. KITAB / OpenITI work on detecting chains in classical Arabic texts

### Takeaway
KITAB (Ryan Muther and David Smith, Northeastern University, ERC grant 772989) built a CRF chain detector for long, unpunctuated OpenITI texts using expert human-in-the-loop annotation. Partial-overlap F1 is .835 but exact-span F1 is only .254, and start-point detection peaked at F1 .476 with a retrieve-and-tag model. The code, two trained CRF models and the annotated training chunks are public under MIT, and the detector's output fed later KITAB datasets.

### Cited Findings
- Task and data: chains in 4,285 OpenITI texts (1.5 billion words), punctuation removed and orthography normalized. Training data: 50 texts, 163 tagged regions, 907,110 tokens, 3,071 chains, average chain length 31.9 tokens. Texts were chosen over several rounds in which a CRF was trained, its errors reviewed by scholars, and new texts picked to cover the failures. ([Muther & Smith 2020](https://aclanthology.org/2020.wanlp-1.12.pdf))
- Inter-annotator study: five annotators, the same 2,000 lines; Krippendorff's α 0.84; full agreement on start points 70% of the time and on end points 53%; outliers were mostly two adjacent chains merged; about 3% of chains begin right after another ends. ([Muther & Smith 2020](https://aclanthology.org/2020.wanlp-1.12.pdf))
- Models: CRF with windowed token-count features; CRF with 200-dimensional GloVe trained on OpenITI; BiLSTM over chunks of at most 1,000 words; retrieve-and-tag (bag-of-ngrams logistic regression retrieves chunks likely to hold a chain start, then a single-layer LSTM tags the start). Only 30% of 100-token chunks contain a chain start. ([Muther & Smith 2020](https://aclanthology.org/2020.wanlp-1.12.pdf))
- Span results for the token CRF (Table 2, 80/10/10 split): exact P .270, R .241, F1 .254; partial P .873, R .804, F1 .835. ([Muther & Smith 2020](https://aclanthology.org/2020.wanlp-1.12.pdf))
- Start-point results (Table 3, token-level "Begin" tag, micro-averaged over ten document-level splits): naive baseline .188/.378/.219; CRF tokens .475/.422/.432; Retrieval-LSTM .571/.442/.476. The naive baseline treats every common start term as a chain start; the footnote lists حدثنيه، حدثنا، سئل and قال. Best retrieval upper-bound recall .959 (bigrams, TF-IDF, 75-token chunks, threshold 0.14). ([Muther & Smith 2020](https://aclanthology.org/2020.wanlp-1.12.pdf))
- Known failure: an early model labelled lists of students in biographies as chains; new training texts were picked to correct it. Future work named: a gazetteer of transmission terms, NER features, and a model for end points. ([Muther & Smith 2020](https://aclanthology.org/2020.wanlp-1.12.pdf))
- KITAB blog (Ryan Muther, 3 February 2020): word-level tenfold cross-validation of the word-tagging model gives P .853, R .805, F1 .819. Applied to Ibn Asakir's Ta'rikh madinat Dimashq "(800,000 words in total)", it found "just under 91,000 isnads, comprising about 40% of the text". Those two figures do not fit together (91,000 chains at about 32 tokens each exceed 800,000 words), so one of them is probably mistyped. The blog also flags قال as ambiguous between a transmission term and a quotation marker. ([KITAB blog](https://kitab-project.org/Tracking-Traditions-Identifying-Isnads-in-the-OpenITI-Corpus/))
- Code: mutherr/isnadDetectionCode (created February 2020, MIT, last push 14 October 2026) ships `tokenIsnadModel_28-7-20.crf` and `tokenIsnadModel_27-11-19.crf`, training and tagging scripts, BIO tags B_Isnad/I_Isnad/O, and the annotation markers @Isnad_Beg@ / @Isnad_End@ (written back as @ISB@ / @ISE@). ([GitHub](https://github.com/mutherr/isnadDetectionCode))
- Use at scale: the classifier was run in July 2020 on OpenITI release 2020.1.2 for the "People versus Books" dataset, whose notes say "Almost all of the isnāds start with a transmissive term" and call the named-entity list "a very first effort". ([Zenodo](https://zenodo.org/records/5074633))
- Follow-up on names (Muther, Smith & Savant, Journal of Historical Network Research 8, 2023): GigaBERT NER for name mentions inside chains scored P/R/F1 .99/.99/.99 when trained on the classical chain data (tenfold) and .95/.97/.96 when trained on the modern ANERCorp; the authors used the modern model because it should generalize better. Fine-tuned mention embeddings plus community detection (Leiden, label propagation) cluster mentions of the same person; the best clustering "reduces error on the CoNLL metric by 30%". ([JHNR article](https://jhnr.net/articles/10.25517/jhnr.v8i1.135); [code](https://github.com/mutherr/isnadNameDisambiguation))
- Later KITAB papers moved to attributing quoted passages to the earlier works they came from, using language models (Muther & Barber 2023, CEUR; Muther & Smith 2023, arXiv 2306.17322). ([CORDIS results list](https://cordis.europa.eu/project/id/772989/results/it))

### Inferences
- The KITAB detector targets chains embedded in running prose (history, tafsir, biography). Hadith collections already split entries, so it complements rather than replaces an entry-level splitter.
- Its exact-boundary weakness (F1 .254) and the annotators' disagreement on end points and merged chains match the team's problem: finding where one chain stops and the next starts is the hard part.
- The released training chunks (907K tokens, 3,071 chains, MIT) are the largest openly licensed hand-annotated chain-span data found, though they mark only chains, not matns or comments.

### Gaps
- No KITAB publication after 2020 reports new chain-detection metrics (for example with transformers); the 2026 repository push suggests maintenance without a new paper.
- Annotation guidelines for the KITAB chain markup were not found.

## 4. Narrator name extraction and disambiguation, linking to biographical dictionaries, and transmission-term detection

### Takeaway
Finding narrator mentions inside a known chain is close to solved on in-domain data (F1 0.96 to 0.99 for BERT-family taggers). Linking mentions to the right person is not: AR-Sanad's AraBERT falls from 92.9 micro-F1 on artificial chains to 83.5 on real ones, with 60.6% of real chains containing at least one wrong identity, and most linking work is tested on artificial or small sets. I found no peer-reviewed system with measured accuracy that links narrators to Tahdhib al-Kamal or Taqrib. Transmission terms are handled as gazetteers or tag classes inside chain taggers and are rarely evaluated on their own.

### Cited Findings

#### Mention extraction in Arabic
- Oumer 2025: narrator recognition F1 0.98 (CAMeLBERT-CA), 0.97 (AraT5), 0.84 (AraBART) on 33,523 test hadiths. ([GitHub README](https://github.com/JehadOumer/Automatic-Hadith-Isnad-Parsing-Models))
- Muther, Smith & Savant 2023: GigaBERT NER F1 .99 (classical training) and .96 (ANERCorp training) on chains from Ta'rikh Dimashq. ([JHNR article](https://jhnr.net/articles/10.25517/jhnr.v8i1.135))
- Balgasem & Zakaria 2017: keyword-triggered rules for name start and end, then statistical measures (LLR, PMI, S-cost, R-cost, U-cost); "86% of f-measure for the rule-based method", LLR "85% of precision". Secondary sources give 76% F1 ([TELKOMNIKA review](https://telkomnika.uad.ac.id/index.php/TELKOMNIKA/article/download/27447/12280)) and 82% F-measure on 235 al-Bukhari hadiths ([Najeeb 2022, related work](https://doi.org/10.1155/2022/7160509)). ([Balgasem & Zakaria abstract](https://doi.org/10.1109/ICEEI.2017.8312417))
- Alhawarat 2015: n-grams and simple rules for Arabic person names over the six collections, F-measure 65.11% (n-grams) and 70.76% (rules), per [Najeeb 2022](https://doi.org/10.1155/2022/7160509) and the [TELKOMNIKA review](https://telkomnika.uad.ac.id/index.php/TELKOMNIKA/article/download/27447/12280).
- Makhlouta, Zaraket & Harkous 2012: morphology, finite-state machines and graph transformations; "F-score 98%" for chains and narrators on Ibn Hanbal's Musnad, per the [TELKOMNIKA review, Table 4](https://telkomnika.uad.ac.id/index.php/TELKOMNIKA/article/download/27447/12280) (primary not checked).
- Wessam et al. 2025: AraBERT, LSTM and AraBERT-LSTM on the "Hadith Noor dataset" with eight entity types including narrators and places; the hybrid reaches "accuracy of 0.981" (metric level not stated). ([abstract](https://doi.org/10.54216/fpa.180117))
- Narrator tagging on translations, less relevant to Arabic: Luthfi et al. 2022, BERT on Indonesian (102 al-Bukhari hadiths, F1 99.63%, 98.27% on other passages) ([IJACSA](https://doi.org/10.14569/IJACSA.2022.0130173)); Alias et al. 2023, Malay rule-based POS tagging (700 development and 300 test documents, recall 93.64%, precision 96.72%) ([AiDAS 2023](https://doi.org/10.1109/AIDAS60501.2023.10284594)).

#### Disambiguation and linking
- AR-Sanad 280K (Mahmoud et al. 2022): disambiguation as 18,298-way classification; fine-tuned AraBERT best, "92.9 Micro F1 score and 30.2 sanad error rate (SER)" on artificial validation chains; on a real test set from the six collections "83.5 Micro F1 score and 60.6 sanad error rate". ([abstract via OpenAlex](https://api.openalex.org/works/https://doi.org/10.3390/info13020055); [MDPI](https://www.mdpi.com/2078-2489/13/2/55))
- Mosa 2025 (Digital Scholarship in the Humanities): a knowledge graph of the narrator network proposes candidates, a transformer scores them; "97.8% accuracy" on AR-Sanad 280K-v2. ([doi](https://doi.org/10.1093/llc/fqaf088))
- Mosa 2026 (ACM TALLIP), EviLink-NA: open-set linking that can abstain; candidates from spelling and phonetic normalization, graph anchoring and a bi-encoder; five evidence channels (textual fit, dates, places, consistency with the narrator network, reliability priors); a factor graph enforces joint consistency when several mentions in one chain are ambiguous. Claims state-of-the-art Accuracy@1 and better calibration; the abstract gives no numbers. ([doi](https://doi.org/10.1145/3811820))
- Wirahman 2026 (arXiv 2607.05424, Zenodo): links Sanadset's 185,216 name forms to the Hawramani Hadith Transmitters encyclopedia (100,915 entries): 94,628 links (51.1%; 39,938 HIGH, 54,690 MED); then Hawramani to muslimscholars.info (25,247 entries): 95,573 links (94.7%), scored 50% name similarity, 40% death-year proximity, 10% grade polarity. No precision or recall is reported. ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.05424); [Zenodo](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.21019692))
- Narrator2Vec (Mghari et al. 2024): 94% accuracy on top-10 narrator prediction, per the [TELKOMNIKA review](https://telkomnika.uad.ac.id/index.php/TELKOMNIKA/article/download/27447/12280).
- ABCD (Ramzy et al. 2023): classifying hadiths by the narrator they go back to; deep learning on chain input 92% F1, machine learning on matn input 77% F1. ([doi](https://doi.org/10.3390/bdcc7030141))
- Maraoui et al. 2022: a TEI narrator encyclopedia built from Wikipedia articles with NER; results described only as "encouraging". ([doi](https://doi.org/10.13053/cys-26-3-4349))
- Narrator databases usable for linking: Itqan (Zenodo 2026) with 65,391 narrator profiles and jarh wa ta'dil parsed from 8 classical texts ([Zenodo](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.19478684)); faisalkindi/isnad uses an Itqan corpus of "115k narrators, 22 source books", 196,488 normalized name forms, trigram retrieval of 12 candidates per position, then Claude picks one using dates, tabaqat and neighbouring narrators, with no evaluation ([GitHub](https://github.com/faisalkindi/isnad)); hadith-kg's 49,819 biographies and 127,863 critic statements ([card](https://huggingface.co/datasets/emadjumaah/hadith-kg)).

#### Transmission-term detection
- Najeeb 2022, hybrid model per-category accuracy: Received-Method 85%, Received-Method-Prefix 83%, Narrator-Name 92%, Narrator-Name-Prefix 90%, Prophet-Name 96%, Title 73%, Replacement 84%, Others 87%; phrases were pre-cut by an expert. ([Najeeb 2022](https://doi.org/10.1155/2022/7160509))
- Muther & Smith 2020: start-term baseline (حدثنيه، حدثنا، سئل، قال) P .188, R .378, F1 .219 for chain starts. ([paper](https://aclanthology.org/2020.wanlp-1.12.pdf))
- Oumer: NER tags cover narrators and optionally narration words; adding narration words such as حدثنا and أخبرنا to the relation tuples is listed as future work, and only narrator scores are published. ([SIGARAB post](https://groups.google.com/g/sigarab/c/XFnOZYvgAfg); [README](https://github.com/JehadOumer/Automatic-Hadith-Isnad-Parsing-Models))
- Savant & Seydi used ḥaddathanī/ḥaddathanā to find al-Tabari's direct teachers across three of his works. ([Zenodo](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.5533889))
- AhmadAkrabawe/isnad-modifying (2024) strips a fixed list of 26 formula words (عن، حدثنا، أخبرنا، أن، قال، سمعت …) and needs a comma after each name. ([GitHub](https://github.com/AhmadAkrabawe/isnad-modifying_v0.1))

### Inferences
- The artificial-to-real drop (micro-F1 92.9 to 83.5; chains with an error 30.2% to 60.6%) means chain-level linking errors are common. For ICMA bundles, where one wrong identity moves a chain to a different narrator, chain-level error rate is the number to track, not mention-level F1.
- Mention finding inside a clean chain is a solved enough subtask to reuse (Oumer's models, or KITAB's NER recipe). Linking is where the team should expect to spend effort and to hand-check.
- No paper evaluates per-link formula classification (حدثنا versus عن versus أخبرنا) as its own task, although every system relies on those words.

### Gaps
- No measured linking against Tahdhib al-Kamal or Taqrib found; the closest are databases built from rijal works (Itqan, hadith-kg) without published linking accuracy.
- AR-Sanad 280K-v2 with NarratorsKG (Mahmoud, Nabil, Saif & Torki, Neural Computing and Applications 2024, [doi](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00521-024-10194-2)): abstract not retrieved; a search summary claims 94.6% accuracy on the original validation set, unverified.
- EviLink-NA's actual scores were not retrieved.

## 5. Work on detecting collector formulas (مثله، نحوه، بمعناه، بهذا الإسناد), collector comments and narrators' remarks

### Takeaway
I found no published work that detects collector formulas or labels the collector's comments and narrators' remarks as separate classes with an evaluation. Existing systems and datasets meet these cases only incidentally: they drop them, merge them into one of two spans, or copy them from a website field.

### Cited Findings
- LK keeps entries that are only a chain ending "بهذا الإسناد" as records with no matn; the collector's grading comment sits in Arabic_Comment, taken from sunnah.com's layout. ([LREC 2020](https://aclanthology.org/2020.lrec-1.415.pdf))
- Altammami's 2020 rule handles one extra chain before the matn (split point B); the thesis HMM fails on entries with "several Isnad and Matn segments" and the author calls for finer segments. ([LREC 2020](https://aclanthology.org/2020.lrec-1.415.pdf); [thesis](https://etheses.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/32802/1/Altammami_SH_Computing_PhD_2023.pdf))
- Harrag's FST extracted "Taalik" and "Atraf" alongside Isnad and Matn, relying on one edition's punctuation. ([Altammami thesis](https://etheses.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/32802/1/Altammami_SH_Computing_PhD_2023.pdf))
- The NAH corpus stores "Authors Comments" separately; in one of its collections the hadith type (e.g. مرفوعا) is written at the end of the chain and confused the PPM splitter. ([Tarmom et al. 2020](https://aclanthology.org/2020.icon-main.4.pdf))
- KITAB annotators disagreed most on end points and on merging adjacent chains; about 3% of chains begin right after another ends. ([Muther & Smith 2020](https://aclanthology.org/2020.wanlp-1.12.pdf))
- Oumer's chain-aware triplets add a chain number to each teacher-student link (AraT5 F1 0.93), the only evaluated method found that keeps several chains in one entry apart. ([README](https://github.com/JehadOumer/Automatic-Hadith-Isnad-Parsing-Models))
- Multi-IsnadSet records different chains of one hadith separately but does not describe how they were split. ([Europe PMC](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC11096860/fullTextXML))
- faisalkindi/isnad claims LLM handling of ح, joint narration (قالا) and relative references (عن أبيه), without evaluation. ([GitHub](https://github.com/faisalkindi/isnad))
- Local checks: hadith-kg leaves follow-up chains, "مثله/بمثله" formulas and collector remarks outside its single matn span and unlabelled (examples in question 2); Sanadset tags a three-chain ح entry entirely as MATN and flattens parallel teachers into one chain. ([hadith-kg](https://huggingface.co/datasets/emadjumaah/hadith-kg); [Sanadset mirror](https://huggingface.co/datasets/arbml/Sanadset))
- The June 2026 LLM-era review does not discuss collector formulas and says computational work on commentary (sharh, takhrij) "remains sparse". ([arXiv 2608.20364 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.20364))
- An Arabic-language search for automatic extraction of chain and matn with تحويل، مثله، نحوه returned nothing relevant. (Search performed 19 October 2026; no citable result.)

### Inferences
- The team's label set (several chains, several matns, formulas, collector comments, narrator remarks) has no prior benchmark or annotation scheme to adopt. It will need its own guidelines and a hand-checked gold set.
- hadith-kg's per-entry chain table (96,163 of the 403,368 entries that have chains carry two or more; local check) and narrator mention offsets can bootstrap chain spans; formula, comment and remark labels will have to be created.
- Formula phrases form a short, closed list, so a rule-based tagger checked on a sample is a reasonable first baseline; the literature offers no numbers to compare it with.

### Gaps
- Harrag's treatment of Taalik/Atraf could not be checked in the primary.
- Najeeb's "Replacement" and "Title" categories are not defined in the text I read, so whether they cover formulas like بهذا الإسناد is unknown.

### Inferences
- The choice of what to reuse depends on the subtask: KITAB's CRF for finding chains in prose; Oumer's CAMeLBERT or AraT5 for narrator mentions and links inside chain text; SHK4K or an Altammami-style bigram model as single-boundary baselines; LK's test set and KITAB's chunks as outside evaluation data.
- Missing or unnamed licences (LK, Oumer, the KITAB name-disambiguation repository) need checking before any redistribution.
