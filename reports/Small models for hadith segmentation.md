# Small Arabic taggers can learn matn boundaries

A small model trained on the project's own labels suits this task, and the best fit is smaller than the 0.8B or 4B decoders in the question: an Arabic encoder of 0.1B to 0.3B parameters, a model that reads the whole entry at once, fine-tuned to give every word a label such as chain, link to the Prophet, matn, collector's formula or collector's comment. The existing rules would stay in charge of the closed formula lists and of deciding which chain each wording belongs to. The project's misses are choices about where a matn starts and ends that turn on nearby words, which trained word taggers handle well, while prompting a far larger general model gained two points (DeepSeek V4 Pro right on 63% of chains against the rules' 61%). In published head-to-head tests, fine-tuned encoders score within 2 to 5 points (on the usual 100-point F1 scale for labelling) of fine-tuned decoders 20 to 60 times their size and came out slightly ahead on Arabic entity tagging; they also return exact character positions without copying the entry, and would tag all of hadith-kg in under half an hour on one consumer GPU. The strongest non-American encoders are CAMeLBERT-CA (pretrained on classical Arabic; built at NYU Abu Dhabi, the UAE campus of an American university), Rawi (Algeria; reads 8,192 tokens; non-commercial licence) and NeoAraBERT (Qatar and Lebanon; the only one built to read vowel marks). If a decoder is tried, Qwen3.5-4B-Base (China, Apache 2.0) has the best measured Arabic at its size and spends half of Qwen3's tokens on vocalized hadith. The binding constraint is labels: no public dataset marks several chains, formulas and comments inside one entry, hadith-kg's own matn span is exact on only 11 of 21 test entries, and a trained model inherits its labeller's habits. Blind Claude labels cost about $180 per 10,000 entries through the batch service, a hand check of a few hundred chains is still needed, and each training run costs a few dollars at most.

## Most misses sit at the edges of a matn, where collectors' phrasing varies

For every chain an entry lists, the project needs the exact words that are that chain's matn, because the ICMA step later compares the wording each chain gives with the places where the chains split. The rules fix those words with closed lists: they skip the link to the Prophet, read formulas such as مثله، نحوه and بمعناه as a claim about another chain's words, and end a matn at markers such as قال أبو داود or at the start of the next chain ([rules document](../docs/chain-wording.md)). On the 81 fresh entries of round 2, the only fair test, the rules get **92 of 151 chains in multi-chain entries exactly right (61%)**. DeepSeek V4 Pro, given the same rules as its prompt, gets **50 of 79 (63%)** on the 30 entries it finished, where the rules get 49 ([project evaluation](../eval/chain-wording-2026-10-18.md)). Two blind Claude labellers agree on **55 of 60 chains (92%)**, so the rules are precise enough for a careful reader to apply. The basis (own, same, similar and the rest) is right for 150 of 172 chains. The misses sit mostly at matn edges: links to the Prophet phrased in ways the lists lack (يرفعه إلى النبي ﷺ قال), comment markers (كذا قال، متن غريب، قال شيخنا، قال البزار) and remarks that introduce further chains. One round of fixes lifted unseen accuracy from 41% to 61%, and each fresh sample brought forms the vocabulary did not have ([project evaluation](../eval/chain-wording-2026-10-18.md)).

The two systems also fail in different places. Where they agree, on 42 of 79 chains, they are right on 39 (93%). On the 37 where they disagree, only the rules are right on 10, only DeepSeek on 11, and neither on 16 ([project evaluation](../eval/chain-wording-2026-10-18.md)). A list grows one phrase at a time, and a prompted model, however large, has read the rules without seeing a single labelled entry. A model fine-tuned on labelled entries learns from examples which words end a matn in context, including unmarked comments such as al-Naḥḥās's remark straight after a matn, which the rules knowingly keep inside it ([rules document](../docs/chain-wording.md)). On Arabic tagging, fine-tuning beats prompting by wide margins: a fine-tuned AraBERTv2 scored 0.86 F1 on nested entities in literary prose, against 0.59 for the best few-shot LLM ([AdabNER](https://aclanthology.org/2026.acl-long.1541/)).

An invented entry shows the job. The collector's teacher Bakr passes on a hadith from Zayd, a second teacher, Khalid, passes on the same hadith from Zayd, and the collector closes with a remark:

| Words of the entry | Meaning | Label |
|---|---|---|
| حدثنا بكر عن زيد | Bakr told us, from Zayd | chain |
| عن النبي ﷺ قال: | from the Prophet, who said: | link to the Prophet |
| «...» | the Prophet's words | matn |
| ح وحدثنا خالد عن زيد | (ḥ, the mark of a new chain) and Khalid told us, from Zayd | chain |
| بمثله | with the same words | collector's formula |
| هذا حديث غريب | this hadith is rare | collector's comment |

The tagger supplies the third column. The rules already do the next step well: they give Khalid's chain the basis `same` and point it at Bakr's wording. Labelling each word where it stands also covers two rare structures the rules miss, a matn split into two pieces and a matn given before its chain, because the rules draw one stretch of words per chain ([rules document](../docs/chain-wording.md); [project evaluation](../eval/chain-wording-2026-10-18.md)).

A real entry from the ʿUbāda set shows what rides on these labels. In Muslim's Ṣaḥīḥ (hadith-kg entry 10237), Muslim's teachers Muḥammad b. al-Muthannā and Ibn Bashshār pass on the hadith through Saʿīd and Qatāda, and the matn opens with the revelation scene, كان نبي الله ﷺ إذا أنزل عليه كرب لذلك. Three more chains follow, through Shuʿba and through Hishām, closed by كلاهما عن قتادة بهذا الإسناد، غير أن في حديثهما ("both from Qatāda with this chain, except that in their hadith ..."). Then come the words those chains give, البكر يجلد وينفى والثيب يجلد ويرجم, and Muslim's own remark, لا يذكران سنة ولا مائة ("they mention neither the year nor the hundred"). hadith-kg's matn span for this entry starts at خُذُوا عَنِّي, which drops the revelation scene, and ends before the chains that follow, which leaves their shorter wording and Muslim's remark with no label ([hadith-kg, local copy](../data/raw/hadith-kg/hadith-kg.db)). The blind gold labels start the shared matn at كان نبي الله and give the three following chains only the short wording ([ʿUbāda gold labels](../eval/gold/chain-wording/ubada-khudhu-anni.json)). Both details carry Pavlovitch's argument. He first divides the hadith by whether the revelation scene is present, finds Shuʿba to be the common link for the wordings without it, and places Shuʿba's form, with bare verbs and no numbers, in the second quarter of the second century AH (Pavlovitch 2011, pp. 164 and 181; [project summary](../eval/gold/ubada-pavlovitch-2011.md)). A segmenter that drops the scene or folds Muslim's remark into the matn erases the evidence the method needs.

## Published hadith splitters stop at one chain and one matn

Since 2010, chain-and-matn splitters have tried rules, word-pair lists, hidden Markov models and compression models on 100 to 500 hadiths, mostly from the six canonical collections, with metrics that cannot be compared across papers. Altammami's widely cited **92.5%** (222 of 240 hadiths from six collections) counts a split as right when it is off by fewer than four words ([Altammami et al. 2019](https://aclanthology.org/W19-5605.pdf); [Altammami thesis 2023](https://etheses.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/32802/1/Altammami_SH_Computing_PhD_2023.pdf)). On his 50 irregular test hadiths the best system fell to **79.0%**, against 95.1% on regular ones, and his hidden Markov model failed on entries with several chain and matn segments, which led him to conclude that hadith can be split into more than two parts ([Altammami thesis 2023](https://etheses.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/32802/1/Altammami_SH_Computing_PhD_2023.pdf)). A compression-based splitter reached 92.76% word-level accuracy on one corpus and 90.10% on another ([Tarmom et al. 2020](https://aclanthology.org/2020.icon-main.4.pdf)).

Taggers trained on in-domain data do far better on the simple split. pyHadith, a spaCy model trained on 102,153 hadiths, scored **F1 0.983** for chain and matn on 20,430 held-out hadiths (F1 balances missed labels against wrong ones and is written either from 0 to 1 or from 0 to 100), and has since been withdrawn from PyPI ([pyHadith listing](https://libraries.io/pypi/pyHadith)). A 2026 AraBERT tagger on Hugging Face labels chain and matn words but publishes no scores and cuts input at 512 subwords ([SHK4K model card](https://huggingface.co/SHK4K/hadith-segmentation-bert)). The KITAB project's conditional random field tagger, built to find chains inside running prose across OpenITI (a large open corpus of pre-modern Arabic), scores **F1 0.835 when partly overlapping chains count but only 0.254 for exact spans**. Five expert annotators fully agreed on where a chain starts 70% of the time and on where it ends 53% of the time ([Muther and Smith 2020](https://aclanthology.org/2020.wanlp-1.12.pdf)). Its training data, 907,110 tokens holding 3,071 hand-marked chains, is public under the MIT licence and is the largest openly licensed set of hand-marked chains found ([isnadDetectionCode](https://github.com/mutherr/isnadDetectionCode)).

The neighbouring tasks are further along. A fine-tuned CAMeLBERT-CA finds narrator names inside chains at F1 0.98 on a 33,523-hadith test set ([Oumer 2025](https://github.com/JehadOumer/Automatic-Hadith-Isnad-Parsing-Models)), while linking those names to people remains hard: an AraBERT linker fell from 92.9 to 83.5 micro-F1 when moved from artificial to real chains, and 60.6% of the real chains held at least one wrong identity ([AR-Sanad 280K](https://www.mdpi.com/2078-2489/13/2/55)). On entity tagging in al-Bukhārī (CANERCorpus, 7,000 hadiths), all ten fine-tuned encoders scored over 96% F1, CAMeLBERT-CA highest at 97.78, on a random split that lets the same narrators appear in training and test ([Muhammed and Azab 2025](https://thesai.org/Downloads/Volume16No10/Paper_19-Evaluating_Transformer_Based_Pretrained_Models.pdf)); off-the-shelf Arabic entity models had scored poorly on new al-Bukhārī material ([Alshammari et al. 2023](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/211347)). The Rezwan corpus used prompted LLMs to separate chain from matn across 1.2 million narrations, and six experts rated the separation 9.33 out of 10, with no boundary-level accuracy given ([Rezwan](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.03781)). In the Qur'an QA 2023 shared task, fine-tuned encoders won answer-span extraction from Qur'an passages ([Qur'an QA 2023 overview](https://aclanthology.org/2023.arabicnlp-1.76)). In IslamicEval 2025, where systems had to find Qur'an verses and hadiths inside chatbot answers by character position, LLM systems took the top three places (90.06, 87.20 and 86.11 macro F1), and the Isnad AI entry, an AraBERTv2 tagger trained only on template-generated examples, scored 66.97 ([IslamicEval overview](https://aclanthology.org/2025.arabicnlp-sharedtasks.67); [Isnad AI model card](https://huggingface.co/FatimahEmadEldin/Isnad-AI-Identifying-Islamic-Citation)). That tagger never saw a real labelled answer, so the gap mixes the effect of architecture with the effect of synthetic labels, and it warns against training on rule-made examples alone.

Formulas and comments have no published treatment. No system found detects collector formulas or labels collector comments with an evaluation, and a 2026 survey calls computational study of commentary sparse ([Haque and Islam 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.20364)). The large datasets do not fill the gap. hadith-kg stores one matn span per entry, inherited from markup in a 2017 database dump, and in **202,664 of its 714,610 spanned entries (28%) more than 20 characters follow the span**, holding further chains, formulas and comments with no label ([hadith-kg card](https://huggingface.co/datasets/emadjumaah/hadith-kg); [local copy](../data/raw/hadith-kg/hadith-kg.db)). Sanadset 650K tagged chains with regular expressions and published no checks. In one of its three data files, 75,905 of 216,995 rows carry no chain tag although most contain transmission verbs, and an al-Ṭabarī entry with three chains joined by ح is tagged wholly as matn ([Sanadset mirror](https://huggingface.co/datasets/arbml/Sanadset); [Sanadset paper](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9440281/)). The LK corpus (39,038 hadiths from six collections) is hand-checked only for al-Bukhārī; checks of the first 50 hadiths in each of the other collections found 7.6% errors, and its repository has no licence file ([LK corpus](https://github.com/ShathaTm/LK-Hadith-Corpus); [Altammami thesis 2023](https://etheses.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/32802/1/Altammami_SH_Computing_PhD_2023.pdf)). The datasets also disagree on convention: Tarmom moved "the Prophet ... said" from matn to chain, while Sanadset keeps قال رسول الله in the matn ([Tarmom et al. 2020](https://aclanthology.org/2020.icon-main.4.pdf); [Sanadset mirror](https://huggingface.co/datasets/arbml/Sanadset)). Mixing them without one convention adds noise.

## Encoders of 0.1B to 0.3B keep pace with decoders twenty times larger

An encoder reads the whole entry at once and gives each word piece a label in one pass. A decoder, the kind of model behind chat assistants, writes its answer one token (word piece) at a time, so for this task it must copy the entry with tags inserted or point at positions in it. On supervised span labelling the encoder holds its ground. On English news entities, RoBERTa-large (355M parameters) scores 0.900 F1, while 7B Llama 2 and Mistral models fine-tuned as word classifiers score 0.744 and 0.756 when they keep their left-to-right attention and 0.908 to 0.927 once that restriction is lifted, a gain of 2 to 3 points for 20 times the parameters ([Dukić and Šnajder 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2401.14556v1)). Across 20 supervised entity datasets, a fine-tuned 7B decoder averaged 84.78 F1 against 82.9 for a 0.3B encoder and 80.09 for BERT-base ([UniversalNER](https://arxiv.org/html/2308.03279); [GLiNER](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.08526v1)). For Arabic, a WojoodNER 2024 team fine-tuned BLOOMZ-7b, Gemma-7b and AraBERTv2 and found that the three "performed similarly with AraBERTv2 favored" ([DRU at WojoodNER 2024](https://aclanthology.org/2024.arabicnlp-1.104/)). In biomedical tagging, 7B and 8B decoders tuned with QLoRA (which trains a small set of added weights on a compressed, frozen copy of the model) beat 0.3B to 0.4B encoders on four of five datasets, by margins such as 90.73 against 88.86 on BC5CDR, while the encoders ran 40 to 220 times faster per example ([Obeidat et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.00664v1)).

Decoders that rewrite their input drift from it, even after fine-tuning. A generative entity tagger that regenerated every input word still dropped, added and "corrected" words, and omissions made 39% of its mismatches ([GNER](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.16602v2)). Llama 3 8B and Command R, told to insert sentence breaks and change nothing else, altered about 1.5% and 2% of the characters ([SaT](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.16678v2)). Sadeed, a 1.5B decoder fine-tuned to add vowel marks to mostly classical Arabic, lost about 7.19 of its 9.92 word-error points to words it invented while rewriting ([Sadeed](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.21635v2)). Vocalized hadith, with its hamza forms and spelling variation, gives a decoder the same room to drift. The fixes add machinery. Constrained decoding that allows only the entry's own tokens plus tags made LLaMA-33B's outputs valid every time instead of 64.2% of the time, though valid output can still be wrong ([Geng et al. 2023](https://aclanthology.org/2023.emnlp-main.674)), and IslamicEval's second-placed system aligned its LLM's bracket tags back to the answer by dynamic programming ([IslamicEval overview](https://aclanthology.org/2025.arabicnlp-sharedtasks.67)). An encoder needs none of this, because the tokenizer's offset map turns each label into exact character positions in the stored entry ([Transformers tokenizer docs](https://huggingface.co/docs/transformers/main_classes/tokenizer)).

The best case for generation comes from essay segmentation, where a QLoRA-tuned Llama-3.1-8B scored 87.52 token-level F1, about 18 points over a Longformer tagger. That metric forgives near-misses, and the paper does not say clearly whether the tagger was retrained on the same split ([Favero et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.14389v2)). Neither that result nor IslamicEval tests exact boundaries against a well-trained Arabic encoder. If a decoder is tried, the evidence favours using it as a word classifier with the left-to-right restriction lifted: seven Gemma, Qwen2.5 and Llama decoders trained that way beat BERT, RoBERTa and DeBERTa, though not the best published systems ([Yano and Takamura 2026](https://aclanthology.org/2026.bionlp-1.25/)). The trick fits models built from standard attention, such as Qwen3. Qwen3.5 builds 18 of its 0.8B model's 24 layers from Gated DeltaNet, which carries a running state from left to right ([Qwen3.5 configs](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3.5-9B/blob/main/config.json)). Those layers read in one direction by construction, so lifting a mask would not let them look ahead; this is an inference from the architecture, and no study has tested it.

Length and speed leave the choice where it is. The median entry has 66 words and the 99th percentile 397 ([local copy](../data/raw/hadith-kg/hadith-kg.db)). AraBERT's tokenizer spends about 1.56 tokens per unvocalized word by the DF-Arc team's own count ([DF-Arc card](https://huggingface.co/dataflare/df-arc/blob/main/README.md)), so 512 tokens hold about 330 words and roughly 1% to 2% of entries pass the 512-token limit of CAMeLBERT and AraBERT. Hugging Face's token-classification pipeline handles those with overlapping windows ([Transformers pipelines docs](https://huggingface.co/docs/transformers/main_classes/pipelines)), and Rawi, AraModernBERT and EuroBERT read 8,192 tokens in one pass. At BERT-base's measured 90,200 tokens per second on an RTX 4090 ([ModernBERT paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.13663)), the corpus's 62 million words, about 93 million tokens, take an estimated 17 minutes. A decoder that rewrites each entry runs tens of times slower per entry and still finishes overnight, so speed is a convenience. The choice rests on the encoder matching the decoder's accuracy while giving exact positions for free.

Its input pipeline also stems words, inserts `[+]` separators and moves marks after each bare word, with no published map back to the original characters, and whether it loads in the stock transformers library is unconfirmed, so it suits a later test of whether vowel marks help. The American encoders belong in the comparison only as controls.

QIMMA, TII's April 2026 leaderboard of 46 models, puts Qwen3.5-4B-Base ahead of every other model of 4B or less it tested, including the American Gemma-3-4b-it and Phi-4-mini at 46.93 each, while the instruct Qwen3.5-0.8B scores 33.03. The QIMMA figures were read from the paper's PDF tables and have not been spot-checked. Such leaderboards test knowledge questions in modern Arabic, and ranks shift between tasks: Qwen3-8B-Base sits near the top on ArabicMMLU and last of its group on Arabic grammar ([Hala-9B card](https://huggingface.co/hammh0a/Hala-9B/blob/main/README.md)). They rule out weak models without ranking strong ones for segmentation. Size counts against the smallest decoders in a less obvious way. About 254M of Qwen3.5-0.8B's parameters sit in its 248,320-entry embedding table, which leaves far less capacity for the task than the name suggests ([Qwen3.5 configs](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3.5-9B/blob/main/config.json)), and NuMind found a 0.5B decoder "too small to be multilingual" for extraction ([NuExtract 1.5](https://numind.ai/blog/nuextract-1-5-multilingual-infinite-context)). If a decoder is tried, 2B to 4B is the useful range. The Arab-world 7B to 9B models lead QIMMA's group of 9B and under within two points of each other, with Qwen3.5-9B (56.28) among them, but read only 4,096 to 8,192 tokens.

Qwen3.5 brings extra setup. Its small models ship as vision-language checkpoints whose language-only model class must be loaded, they need the Hugging Face transformers library at release 5 with the `fla` and `causal_conv1d` kernels to avoid a slow fallback, and Unsloth advises 16-bit LoRA (a small set of added weights trained on a frozen model) over QLoRA, at about 3 GB of GPU memory for 0.8B and 10 GB for 4B ([transformers Qwen3.5 docs](https://huggingface.co/docs/transformers/model_doc/qwen3_5); [Unsloth Qwen3.5 guide](https://unsloth.ai/docs/models/qwen3.5/fine-tune)). Qwen3 uses standard attention and every trainer supports it, which makes it the lowest-risk decoder and the natural base for the word-classifier test. For a released model, Apache 2.0 bases need only the licence, a NOTICE file and marks on changed files. Falcon adds an attribution statement, Gemma 3 derivatives must pass on Google's use restrictions, and Tencent's Hunyuan grants nothing in the EU or UK and forbids using its outputs to improve other models ([Falcon terms](https://falconllm.tii.ae/falcon-terms-and-conditions.html); [Gemma terms](https://ai.google.dev/gemma/terms); [Hunyuan licence](https://huggingface.co/tencent/Hunyuan-4B-Instruct/blob/main/LICENSE)). Two further American families, IBM's Granite 4.x (Apache 2.0) and Liquid AI's LFM2 and LFM2.5 (a licence capped at USD 10M annual revenue), list Arabic, and no independent Arabic evaluation of them at small sizes was found ([Granite 4.1 card](https://huggingface.co/ibm-granite/granite-4.1-8b); [LFM licence](https://www.liquid.ai/lfm-license)). Two Arab-world releases rest on American bases: Fanar on Gemma 2 and, as far as the Hala paper shows, the small Hala models on LFM2 ([Hala paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.14008)).

### Vowel marks double or triple token counts unless stripped with a position map

hadith-kg is almost wholly vocalized, written with the short-vowel marks (harakat): in a 20,000-entry sample, 91.5% of entries carry at least one mark for every two letters ([local measurement](../research_notes/Small%20models%20for%20hadith%20segmentation/tokcount.py)). Tokenizers treat the marks very differently. Qwen3's pre-split rule ends a letter run at every mark, so حَدَّثَنَا becomes six pieces and nine tokens against three for حدثنا, and across the sample Qwen3 spends **5.10 tokens per word on vocalized entries and 1.69 once marks are stripped**. Qwen3.5 added marks to its letter class, keeps حَدَّثَنَا whole at three tokens, and spends **2.57 against 1.46** ([local measurement](../research_notes/Small%20models%20for%20hadith%20segmentation/tokcount.py)). On modern Arabic, full vocalization costs between 2.2 times (Llama 3.1) and 5.3 times (Jais 2) the tokens of unvocalized writing. Removing the marks that ordinary writing carries left benchmark accuracy unchanged, while adding full vocalization automatically cut it, from 58.3 to 46.0 for Llama-3.1 8B ([Inoue et al. 2026](https://aclanthology.org/2026.findings-eacl.22.pdf)). Most encoders expect unvocalized input. CAMeLBERT-CA was pretrained with marks removed, Rawi's card says fully vocalized input lies outside its training, and only NeoAraBERT reads marks by design ([CAMeLBERT-CA card](https://huggingface.co/CAMeL-Lab/bert-base-arabic-camelbert-ca); [Rawi model card](https://huggingface.co/Al-Sila/Rawi)).

The safe practice is to strip marks inside the tokenizer and keep the map. A Hugging Face fast tokenizer with a Replace normalizer for U+064B to U+0652, U+0670 and the tatweel U+0640 keeps an alignment between the normalized and original characters, so every predicted label lands exactly on the vocalized entry ([tokenizers NormalizedString docs](https://docs.rs/tokenizers/latest/tokenizers/tokenizer/normalizer/struct.NormalizedString.html)). Two shortcuts break this. BERT's accent stripping decomposes أ into ا plus a hamza mark and strips the hamza along with the vowels, turning أ إ آ ؤ ئ into bare letters ([Unicode data for U+0623](https://www.compart.com/en/unicode/U+0623)), and AraBERT's preprocessor returns plain strings with no position map ([AraBERT preprocess.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/aub-mind/arabert/master/preprocess.py)). Whether the marks help a tagger at some boundaries, such as the passive حُدِّثْتُ ("I was told"), is untested. Running NeoAraBERT on vocalized and stripped copies of the same labelled entries would settle it.

## The labels set both the ceiling and the budget

### A trained model matches its labeller and inherits its mistakes

Distillation, training a small model on a large model's labels, succeeds on narrow tasks. GLiNER's 50M-parameter encoder, trained on ChatGPT's entity labels, beat ChatGPT at zero-shot entity tagging (52.7 against 47.5 average F1) ([GLiNER](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.08526v1)). A 7B model trained on ChatGPT's labels beat it 41.7 to 34.9 ([UniversalNER](https://arxiv.org/html/2308.03279)), 220M T5 models trained with PaLM 540B's written explanations beat few-shot PaLM on a reasoning benchmark ([Distilling step-by-step](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.02301)), and NuMind says its 3.8B extractor beat the 70B model that labelled its data, a vendor claim ([NuExtract 1.0](https://numind.ai/blog/nuextract-a-foundation-model-for-structured-extraction)). Each of these compares a fine-tuned small model with a prompted large one. A fine-tuned model learns its labeller's systematic, easy-to-learn errors, and it agrees with the labeller more often than the labeller is right ([Burns et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/html/2312.09390)). LLM labels alone also trail careful human labels. BERT trained on 1,000 GPT-4-labelled sentences scored 0.683 F1 against 0.850 for 1,000 human-labelled ones; training on GPT-4 labels first and human labels second reached 0.869, and blending the two in every epoch only 0.792 ([Huang 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.09282v2)). No peer-reviewed study has distilled LLM labels into an Arabic span model.

For this project the tagger will land near the accuracy of whatever labelled its training data. DeepSeek's 63% and the rules' 61% are too low for that role. Blind Claude labels, which the project already uses as its reference and which agree with each other on 92% of chains, are the realistic choice, and they bring an American model into training even when the deployed tagger is CAMeLBERT-CA or Rawi. A non-American labeller could take the role once it matches the blind gold at about 90% on unseen entries; the only one tested so far, DeepSeek V4 Pro, reached 63%.

### Silver labels help once their systematic errors are repaired

Silver labels are labels made automatically, here hadith-kg's matn spans and the rules' output. A model trained on noisy labels beats them when the noise is missing labels rather than wrong ones: BOND lifted knowledge-base labels on English news from 71.40 to 81.48 F1, against 90.11 for full supervision ([BOND](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2006.15509)). When the labelling rules are poor, the trained model only matches them. Across WRENCH's eight tagging datasets, BERT trained on aggregated weak labels averaged 66.50 against 66.32 for the labels themselves and 84.36 for BERT trained on gold ([WRENCH](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2109.11377)). Pouring a small clean set into a large noisy one can make results worse, and a staged recipe that completes the weak labels, trains with a noise-aware loss and finishes on the clean set fixes that ([NEEDLE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.08977)). Clean examples do more good in training than held back only to choose settings ([Zhu et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.17442)). hadith-kg's spans are the hard kind of noise. Their error is systematic, often dropping the opening of the matn as in the Muslim entry, and they are exact on 11 of 21 single-chain test entries against the rules' 16 ([project evaluation](../eval/chain-wording-2026-10-18.md)). Trained on them raw, a tagger would learn to drop openings. The simplest repair replaces the dataset's matn start with the rules' start where the rules are known to do better. A softer one leaves the words between the two boundaries out of the training loss, and a stricter one keeps only entries where the dataset, the rules and a third signal agree. Training then runs in stages, repaired silver first, Claude-labelled entries next and hand-checked ones last, with checkpoints chosen on a separate clean set.

### A few thousand labelled entries, chosen where the systems disagree

Encoder learning curves on LLM-labelled entity data climb steeply over the first 1,500 to 2,000 sentences and flatten near 2,500 ([Zhang et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.21890)). A hadith entry holds several boundary decisions, so it carries more signal than a sentence, but the project's results show each fresh sample still bringing new phrasing, and rare structures such as al-Bukhārī's nested chains need deliberate coverage. The current gold set holds 186 entries and 424 chains, enough to test on and far too few to train on ([project evaluation](../eval/chain-wording-2026-10-18.md)). Disagreement is a cheap guide to where labels pay: on the 79 chains both systems labelled, labelling only the 37 disagreements would have caught 37 of the 40 chains that either system got wrong. Uncertainty-based active learning reached 99% of full-data F1 with 24.9% of the OntoNotes training data ([Shen et al.](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1707.05928)), but data chosen by one model does not reliably help a different one ([Lowell et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1807.04801)). Selection should therefore use the model that will be deployed, with a random slice in every batch to keep accuracy estimates honest. A learning curve at 500, 1,000, 2,000 and 5,000 Claude-labelled entries, with three training runs at each size, stratified by collection and scored per structure, shows where more labels stop paying.

### Labelling dominates the cost, and training is cheap

Claude Opus 5.5 through the Batch API costs $2 per million input tokens and $10 per million output tokens ([Claude pricing](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/pricing)). With a 10,000-token cached rules prompt, a 1,000-token entry and 1,500 output tokens, one label costs about $0.018, or **about $180 per 10,000 entries**. Output makes four-fifths of the bill, so asking only for the first and last words of each span, as the gold files already do, keeps it small. DeepSeek V4 Pro costs $0.014 an entry ([project evaluation](../eval/chain-wording-2026-10-18.md)). By the usual rule of six operations per parameter per training token ([EleutherAI, Transformer Math 101](https://blog.eleuther.ai/transformer-math/)), one pass over 400,000 entries at a generous 500 tokens each takes about 0.8 A100-hours for a 0.15B encoder and about 7 for LoRA on a 4B decoder. That is under $15 at RunPod's $1.19 an hour for an A100 80GB, and a run on 10,000 labelled entries takes minutes ([RunPod pricing](https://www.runpod.io/pricing)). These are estimates from arithmetic, not measured runs. LoRA on a 4B decoder fits a 24 GB card, while full fine-tuning at 4B needs an 80 GB one ([LLaMA-Factory README](https://github.com/hiyouga/LLaMA-Factory); [Unsloth Qwen3.5 guide](https://unsloth.ai/docs/models/qwen3.5/fine-tune)).

### Testing must be stricter than the current gold set allows

With 151 chains, the rules' 61% carries a 95% interval of about ±8 points, and the two labellers' 92% spans about 82% to 96%. Telling apart two systems 5 points apart, with 80% power, takes about 850 chains when both are scored on the same chains and about 1,450 per system on separate sets (standard interval and sample-size formulas applied to the counts in the [project evaluation](../eval/chain-wording-2026-10-18.md)). Test entries should be split from training by hadith-kg's 20,745 meaning groups, so that no hadith appears on both sides, and some whole collections should be held out to measure transfer to unseen collectors ([hadith-kg card](https://huggingface.co/datasets/emadjumaah/hadith-kg)). Holding out whole groups costs a few points and gives a truer number: AdabNER's F1 fell from 0.86 to 0.83 when whole titles were held out, and CANERCorpus's 97.78 came from a random split ([AdabNER](https://aclanthology.org/2026.acl-long.1541/); [Muhammed and Azab 2025](https://thesai.org/Downloads/Volume16No10/Paper_19-Evaluating_Transformer_Based_Pretrained_Models.pdf)). The 92% agreement is neither a ceiling nor a measure of accuracy. Across more than 350 LLMs, when two models both erred they chose the same wrong answer about 60% of the time, and stronger models shared more errors ([Kim et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.07962)), so two Claude labellers can agree on the same mistake. A hand check of 100 to 200 random chains by a reader trained in hadith, or a second labeller from another model family, bounds the true error rate. The scores worth tracking are exact match per chain and per entry, boundary error in words, and splits by structure and by collection, each with an interval.

## Conclusion

The job the small model has to do is narrower than choosing a model suggests. It does not count chains, choose a basis or decide which chain a wording belongs to; the dataset and the rules already do that, and the rules get the basis right for nearly nine chains in ten. It places boundaries and tells a comment from a matn, a labelling job a 0.1B to 0.3B encoder is built for, and every output can be checked in code: the labelled pieces must tile the entry, each chain piece must contain transmission words, and an `own` chain must have words. That division keeps the structure of each entry decided by code, as the project's staged design intends, and it gives the step two opinions on every boundary. Their agreement marks entries safe to accept and their disagreement marks the few to send to Claude or to a reviewer in the existing review screens, so a tagger that only matches the rules still raises the accuracy of the step. The two opinions are only partly independent when the tagger trains on rule-repaired labels, one more reason to train it mainly on blind Claude labels.

The first experiment is small. Fine-tune CAMeLBERT-CA and Rawi on 1,000 to 2,000 blind Claude labels plus repaired hadith-kg spans, keep round 2's meaning groups out of training, and score both against the rules' 61% on round 2 and then on a fresh frozen set of several hundred chains, since round 2 alone can only show a gap of about ten points or more. If an encoder clears the rules but stalls short of the 92% labeller agreement, more and better-chosen labels come next, and a 2B to 4B Qwen decoder is a later test whose published edge over encoders is a few points. If no encoder clears the rules, the labels or the label scheme need fixing before any larger model is worth trying.
